# Hebrew (he) glossary: AFF notes

Modern Israeli Hebrew, right-to-left (the app sets the direction; no direction marks added), Western digits,
numbers as in English (5,500 ft). Units `ft`, `s`, `m`, `in`, `mph` kept in Latin letters.

## How Hebrew skydiving vocabulary works (read first)

- Israeli sport skydiving (Paradive at Habonim, Skykef at Sde Teyman; Israel Parachuting Association, "איגוד הצניחה
  בישראל") runs AFF on USPA-style programmes. Instructors and jumpers mix Hebrew with English loanwords spoken in
  Hebrew letters: "פיילוט צ'וט", "סליידר", "טוגלים", "רייזרים", "פלייר", "ריגר", "בוקס". No public Hebrew
  federation manual with a full AFF vocabulary was found online.
- The IDF paratrooper school gives a stable Hebrew core used by everyone: מצנח (parachute), חופה (canopy), מיתרים
  (lines), רתמה (harness), מצנח ראשי / רזרבי (main / reserve), מד גובה (altimeter), נחיתה (landing).
- Rule used: the word Israeli jumpers actually say; English loanwords in Hebrew letters where that is the dropzone
  word; English acronyms and malfunction names that students hear in English (AFF, AAD, RSL, MARD, PLF, USPA, SIM,
  biplane, side by side, downplane, bag lock, line-over, horseshoe, lock on, Check in / Check out, Prop, Up, Down,
  Arch) stay in Latin letters.
- Addressing the student: commands use the **infinitive** (לבדוק, למשוך, לנתק), the gender-neutral norm of Israeli
  manuals and checklists. Questions and descriptions use the masculine singular "אתה", the standard default in Hebrew
  instructional text. Owner messages ("write to me") use the plural כתבו לי, the neutral form of Israeli apps.
  Case stories keep the gender of the real person (the Category F student is "she").
- Arrows: in sequences (circle of awareness, altitude ranges) "→" became "←", which reads in the right direction in
  right-to-left text (first item on the right). "1,000 ← 300 ft" = from 1,000 down to 300 ft.

Other notes from the check: "pull" is translated two ways on purpose: "לפתוח" for opening the main (the pull
altitude, "pull now") and "למשוך" for pulling a handle or toggle. "Lock on" and the exit-count words (Check in,
Check out, Prop, Up, Down, Arch) stay in English because instructors say them in English in the door.
