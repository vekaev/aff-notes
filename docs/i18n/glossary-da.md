# Danish (da) glossary: AFF notes

Terms follow Dansk Faldskærms Union (DFU) training material where DFU has a word. Where Danish jumpers keep the
English word (cutaway, flare, slider, AAD, RSL, wave-off, hard deck), the app keeps it too.

Sources:
- DFU Elevhåndbog (updated 28-03-2022): https://dfu.dk/sites/default/files/elevhandbog_2022_28-3-2022.pdf
  (nødprocedure, cut-away-pude, reservehåndtag, sikkerhedsudløser, styreprøve, rullefald, landingsrunde, finale,
  biplane / side-by-side, "seneste beslutningshøjde for nødprocedure er 600 m")
- DFU AFF-elevhåndbog (Dec 2017): https://www.dfu.dk/sites/default/files/aff_elevhaandbog_december_2017.pdf
  (svaj, observationscirkel, dummytræk, "lås fast på højdemåleren", hotel-tjek / tjek ind / tjek ud, exit, trækhøjde,
  håndsignaler, "stræk ben")
- DFU Skærmflyvningsmanual: https://dfu.dk/sites/default/files/skaermflyvningsmanual.pdf
  (medvindsben, sidevindsben, finale, holdeområde, venstrehåndsfinale, flare / flarer)
- DFU AFF-instruktørhåndbog (2015): https://www.dfu.dk/sites/default/files/aff_instruktorhandbog_-_september_2015.pdf

Note: DFU teaches in metres (decision altitude 600 m, landing round 300/200/100 m). The app follows the USPA school
in feet; numbers were kept exactly as in English, only the format changed (5.500 ft).

## Back-translation

30 safety-critical strings, Danish back into English (my own reading of the Danish as written).

1. 12ns35n: "More than 4,000 cutaways a year in the US, and reserves rarely fail: it is hesitation that kills. Unclear canopy? At most 5 s to see it clearly get better; if not, do a cutaway and go to the reserve. Line twists that spin: cut away at once (USPA, 2019). As a student never below 2,500 ft." Matches.
2. 1qgx7no: "First the altitude: above 1,000 ft you cut away; below that only silver. Then schools teach one of two ways. Learn one clean sequence: which does your school teach?" Matches.
3. 1g4aj2u: "Below the hard deck a cutaway gives too little height for the reserve. Pull silver and land with both. Only with a downplane do you cut away at any altitude." Drift fixed: first draft used slang "cuttes væk"; now the standard phrase.
4. csfzg (downplane): "Cut away the main, at any altitude (RSL first if there is time). Then steer the reserve and land normally." Matches.
5. o20ozm (biplane): "Don't cut away. Steer the front one gently with its rear risers, only if needed; leave the back one alone. No flare, PLF." Matches.
6. qzt5o8 (side by side): "Don't cut away. Steer the dominant one (most overhead) gently with its rear risers, only if needed. No flare, PLF." Matches.
7. a2sxer (line twists): "Rear risers apart, kick to untwist; toggles stay stowed until the twists are gone. Two tries, check altitude. Starts to spin? Emergency procedure now." Matches.
8. 6cq5ba: "Emergency procedure now. Don't kick, don't wait: a spin can cost up to 300 ft per turn." Matches.
9. 11c6qqs (pull): "Right hand to the pilot chute handle (little ball at the bottom of the container, right side), left hand over the head for balance. Pull it out and throw it to the side at arm's length, then back into the arch while counting. Can't find it or it won't come out? Two tries or 5 s, then Part IV." Order kept.
10. vizfps: "Two tries or 5 s, whichever comes first. Then your school's emergency procedure: reserve only (USPA SIM) or cutaway, then reserve." Matches.
11. 1rfw90: "Pilot chute wrapped or in tow: emergency procedure now, by 2,500 ft at the latest. The AAD is not a plan." Matches.
12. pw0qw2: "Only two tries. Check altitude between them. Not fixed at 2,500 ft? Emergency procedure." Matches.
13. ch7gwv: "Decision altitude for students is 2,500 ft. 1,000 ft is the hard deck, the lowest point for a cutaway." Matches.
14. jy34w3: "A downplane falls fast. It is the one exception to the 1,000 ft hard deck: cut away the main whatever the altitude." Matches.
15. sed644 (lock on): "Stop all exercises. Look only at the altimeter: from 6,000 ft you have about 3 seconds to 5,500 ft. At 5,500 ft: wave-off and pull. Wave-off and pull together take about 1,000 ft, so start on time." Matches.
16. 6fyuro: "Altitude comes before stability: unstable or spinning at 5,500 ft? Pull anyway." Matches.
17. kxk63: "The AAD fires only very low, at a preset altitude, and the RSL is only a backup. Pulling the handles is always your job: pull silver yourself, at once." Matches. ("reserve" in "only a backup" is "reserve" in Danish; reads naturally as backup in context.)
18. 1vxd7dv (pattern ladder): 2,500 decide where and which side; 2,000 → 1,000 hold over holding point; 900 start downwind; 600 turn onto base (sidevindsben); 300 turn onto final into the wind; about 10 ft flare. Matches; numbers unchanged.
19. 1yu0s5l: "No big turns low. Below 300 ft only small, flat turns." Matches.
20. 11xnfod (low turn): "Already in a low turn? Opposite toggle down to stop the turn and get the canopy back overhead. Then flare from the braked position and get ready for a hard landing (PLF). Hit the ground early? Keep flying the canopy." Order kept.
21. 1b49w3n (flare height): "About 10 ft (3 m) above the ground. Too high: don't let the toggles go back up. Stop, hold what you have, fly straight, finish the flare as you sink, and get ready for PLF." Matches.
22. pk87wj (PLF): "You land on your feet and roll along the side of the body so the impact spreads instead of going into ankles and legs. Feet and knees together, knees slightly bent, chin to chest, elbows in." Matches.
23. tdzub9: "Balls of the feet → calf → thigh → hip → side of the back. Five points, one after another…" Matches.
24. 9mw387: "22% of landing incidents without a turn: flaring too early, too late or not at all… Full flare at ~10 ft, hold it, feet and knees together, feet touch first, PLF every time." Matches.
25. 5alreq (water): "Chest strap loosened and RSL off if there is time, half brakes at about 10 ft, deep breath, in the harness until you are in the water. Then cut away, swim forward out of the harness, leave the gear." Order kept.
26. 55lz2n (power lines): "Hanging: touch nothing, nobody touches you until the power company confirms the power is off and stays off." Matches.
27. 8gmc4m (tree): "Stuck: hold the trunk or a big branch, wait for help, don't climb down." Matches.
28. 1eb2awz (aircraft emergency): "Low: land with the plane. Stay in, helmet and belt on, take the brace position (hands clasped behind the head)." Matches.
29. p397a: "To avoid someone: turn right. Coming at each other head-on? Both turn right." Matches.
30. sig3x4: "The lowest canopy has right of way. That does not make the landing area yours…" Drift fixed: first draft said "har vigepligten på sin side (forkørselsret)", where "vigepligt" means the duty to give way, the opposite meaning. Now only "forkørselsret".

Other fixes during checking:
- 1h67itn: "3-ringssystemet" added a digit the English ("three-ring") did not have and failed the number test; now "Tre-ringssystemet".
- 1vvbt2h: "the five-second rule" link text written as "femsekundersreglen" (no digit), to match the English.
- 7nql6j ("English"): the language's own name in the language menu, so left as "English", not translated.

## Least sure (for a native DFU instructor)

1. "hard deck": kept in English. Is there a DFU word (DFU uses only "seneste beslutningshøjde")?
2. "lave cutaway" vs DFU spelling "cut-away" and "cut-away-pude" for the handle.
3. "dummytræk" for practice touches: DFU AFF uses it for touching the handle, but some clubs may say "øvetræk".
4. "venstrerunde / højrerunde" for left/right-hand pattern (DFU only shows "venstrehåndsfinale").
5. "indtag nødstilling" for "brace" in an aircraft emergency, and "observationscirkel" order wording vs DFU's own order.
