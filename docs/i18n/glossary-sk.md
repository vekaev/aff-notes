# Glossary: Slovak (sk)

Terms as used in Slovak and Czech skydiving (Slovenský národný aeroklub clubs and schools share most terminology with
Czech dropzones; official accident reports use the same words). Slovak skydivers keep many English words (AFF, slider,
RSL, AAD, box, lock on, downplane, line-over, pin check); where they do, the app keeps the English word and adds the
Slovak one in brackets once.

## Back-translation

30 of the most safety-critical strings, Slovak → English by the translator.

1. `104so7e` Tangled canopies: don't cut away. Free what you can, pull in the less-filled canopy hand over hand and do a PLF (USPA SIM 4-1).
2. `107azwz` A level wing is more important than the perfect landing spot. Landing priorities: 1 level wing, fly straight; 2 clear area, even if farther; 3 flare and PLF.
3. `11bwry5` Two tries or 5 s, whichever comes first, then the emergency procedure (USPA Cat A). USPA SIM says reserve; ask whether your school teaches only the reserve, or cutaway first.
4. `11c6qqs` Right hand on the pilot chute handle (small "ball" at the bottom of the container, right), left hand above the head for balance. Pull it out and throw it to the side at arm's length, then back to the arch and count. Can't find it or it won't come out? Two tries or 5 s, then Part IV.
5. `11owloq` A spin gets worse fast: decide, cut away, reserve.
6. `11xnfod` Already in a low turn? Opposite toggle down to stop the turn and get the canopy back overhead. Then flare from these brakes and prepare for a hard landing (PLF). Hit earlier? Keep steering the canopy.
7. `12ns35n` In the US over 4,000 cutaways a year and reserves rarely fail: delay kills. Unclear canopy? At most 5 s to see it clearly improving; if not, cut away and go to the reserve. Spinning line twists: cut away immediately. As a student never below 2,500 ft.
8. `14pdpw7` Unclear canopy: at most 5 s to decide, count out loud. A limit, not waiting.
9. `154vw5m` Your main is bad at 2,100 ft. Can you still cut away?
10. `15abr6m` below it no cutaway: only open the reserve (one exception: downplane).
11. `15pbnlv` Emergency procedure. USPA allows two ways: only the reserve, or cutaway then reserve. Do what your school teaches.
12. `16yhndd` In water: cut away, arms back, swim forward out of the harness.
13. `18qw6t8` Fixable, but only two tries: not fixed by 2,500 ft → emergency procedure →
14. `19au8ek` 300 ft. Below it only small flat corrections, at most 45°. ... Above 1,000 ft: at most 180°. 1,000 → 300 ft: at most 90°.
15. `1b49w3n` About 10 ft (3 m) above the ground. Too high: don't let the toggles up. Stop, hold what you have, fly straight, finish as you sink and prepare for PLF.
16. `1b5grfd` At 2,500 ft you should already have decided; 1,000 ft is the lowest altitude for a cutaway. A jumper with more than 1,200 jumps mixed this up and didn't cut away at 2,100 ft.
17. `1g4aj2u` Below the hard deck a cutaway leaves too little height for the reserve. Pull silver and land with both. Only a downplane is cut away at any altitude.
18. `1qgx7no` First altitude: above 1,000 ft you cut away; below it only silver (USPA ISP). Then schools teach one of two ways. Learn one clean sequence: which does your school teach?
19. `1rfw90` Pilot chute wrapped or in tow: emergency procedure immediately, by 2,500 ft. The AAD is not a plan.
20. `1vxd7dv` 2,500 ft: decide where you fly and which side the pattern is. 2,000 → 1,000 ft: wait over the holding point. 900 ft: start downwind. 600 ft: turn onto base. 300 ft: turn onto final, into the wind. About 10 ft: flare.
21. `1xp4bxq` Never let the toggles back up: the canopy would dive. Hold, fly straight, finish near the ground and prepare for PLF.
22. `39hhji` 5-second rule: 5 s is the upper limit, not waiting. Not clearly better? Emergency procedure, as a student never below 2,500 ft. A spin or a canopy that clearly can't fly gets no 5 s: act immediately.
23. `5alreq` If there's time, loosen the chest strap and disconnect the RSL, half brakes at about 10 ft, deep breath, in the harness until you're in the water. Then cut away, swim forward out of the harness, leave the gear.
24. `6fyuro` Altitude has priority over stability: unstable or turning at 5,500 ft? Pull anyway.
25. `6q10ca` On a roof: collapse the canopy; if the wind drags you, cut away (if you can, disconnect the RSL first).
26. `9ci9yx` Obstacle below 300 ft: half brakes; the smallest flat braked turn that misses it, or go into it feet and knees together, flare and PLF. Never a sharp turn.
27. `csfzg` Cut away the main, at any altitude (if there's time, RSL first). Then steer the reserve and land normally.
28. `jy34w3` A downplane falls fast. It is the only exception to the 1,000 ft hard deck: cut away the main regardless of altitude.
29. `kxk63` The AAD activates only very low, at a set altitude, and the RSL is only a backup. Pulling the handles is always your job: pull silver yourself, immediately.
30. `zx50gg` A biplane flies. Keep both, gently steer the front one with its rear risers, don't touch the rear one, no flare, PLF. A flare can slow the front canopy and the rear one can fly over it.

### Drift found and fixed
- "Heading (horizon)" in the USPA cue (`10632al`) first came out as "smer" only; kept "smer (horizont)" to match.
- "Hips into the wind" on exit means the relative wind, not the ground wind; rendered "do prúdu vzduchu" (into the airflow)
  everywhere, so a student doesn't read it as the surface wind.
- "Getting through AFF and the A-license" first read as a broken phrase; fixed to "Ako prejsť AFF a získať licenciu A".
- "Line-over" first glossed as "prehodená šnúra", then unified to "line-over (šnúra cez vrchlík)" in the heading.
- "Flare" kept one Slovak word throughout ("podrovnanie / podrovnať"); the English "flare" is added once in the chapter heading.
- "Dive" (canopy) is always "rúti sa dole" (diving/plunging), never "strmhlav", which is reserved for the freefall head-down
  dive option in a quiz answer.
