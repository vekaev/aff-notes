# Glossary: Slovenian (sl)

Terms as used in Slovenian parachuting regulations (Pravilnik o padalstvu, issued with the Civil Aviation Agency and
Letalska zveza Slovenije as the umbrella air-sports body) and common Slovenian dropzone usage. Slovenian skydivers keep
many English words (AFF, slider, flare, hackey, cutaway, RSL, AAD, box, lock on, wave-off, downplane); where they do,
the app keeps the English word and gives the Slovenian word once in brackets where it helps.

Sources:
- Pravilnik o padalstvu (Uradni list RS 2016), definitions: glavno padalo, rezervno padalo, AAD ("elektronska ali
  mehanska naprava za samodejno odpiranje rezervnega padala"), RSL, aktiviranje padala, nahrbtnik s sistemom vezi:
  https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2016-01-1368/pravilnik-o-padalstvu

  izredni postopki, tehnika skoka, poznavanje padal; AFF instructor exam "Izredni postopki pri izvajanju skokov AFF"):
  https://www.uradni-list.si/files/RS_-2020-113-02139-OB~P001-0000.PDF
- Pravilnik o licenciranju vojaškega padalskega osebja (2022, AFF course content, exam topics):
  https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2022-01-4215/pravilnik-o-licenciranju-vojaskega-padalskega-osebja

## Back-translation

30 of the most safety-critical strings, Slovenian → English by the translator (key: back-translation). Meaning and
order of actions match the English in all 30.

1. `104so7e` Tangled canopies: don't cut away. Untangle what you can, pull the less-inflated canopy toward you with your hands and land with a PLF (USPA SIM 4-1).
2. `107azwz` A level wing is more important than the perfect landing spot. Landing priorities: 1 level wing, flying straight; 2 a clear area, even if farther; 3 flare and PLF.
3. `11bwry5` Two tries or 5 s, whichever comes first, then the emergency procedure. USPA SIM says reserve; ask whether your school teaches only the reserve or a cutaway first.
4. `11c6qqs` Right hand on the pilot chute handle (small "ball" at the bottom of the container, right side), left hand above the head for balance. Pull it out and throw it to the side at arm's length, then back into the arch while you count. Can't find it or it won't come out? Two tries or 5 s, then Part IV.
5. `11owloq` A spin gets worse fast: decide, cut away, reserve.
6. `11xnfod` Already in a low turn? Opposite toggle down to stop the turn and return the canopy overhead. Then flare from those brakes and prepare for a hard landing (PLF). Hit earlier? Keep flying the canopy.
7. `12ns35n` In the US more than 4,000 cutaways a year, and reserves rarely fail: delay kills. Unclear canopy? At most 5 s to see if it's clearly improving; if not, cut away and go to the reserve. Spinning line twists: cut away immediately. As a student never below 2,500 ft.
8. `14pdpw7` Unclear canopy: at most 5 s to decide, count out loud. A limit, not waiting.
9. `154vw5m` At 2,100 ft your main is bad. Can you still cut away?
10. `15abr6m` below there's no cutaway: pull only the reserve (one exception: downplane).
11. `15pbnlv` Emergency procedure. USPA allows two ways: reserve only, or cutaway and then reserve. Do what your school teaches.
12. `16yhndd` In water: cut away, arms back, swim forward out of the harness.
13. `18qw6t8` Fixable, but only two tries: not fixed by 2,500 ft → EP →
14. `19au8ek` 300 ft. Below this altitude only small flat corrections, up to 45°. ... Above 1,000 ft: up to 180°. 1,000 → 300 ft: up to 90°.
15. `1b49w3n` About 10 ft (3 m) above the ground. Too high: don't let the toggles back up. Stop, hold what you have, fly straight, finish while descending and prepare for PLF.
16. `1b5grfd` At 2,500 ft you should already have decided; 1,000 ft is the lowest altitude for a cutaway. A jumper with over 1,200 jumps mixed this up and didn't cut away at 2,100 ft.
17. `1g4aj2u` Below the lower limit (hard deck) a cutaway leaves too little height for the reserve. Pull the silver handle and land with both. Only a downplane is cut away at any altitude.
18. `1qgx7no` First altitude: above 1,000 ft you cut away; below it only the silver handle (USPA ISP). Then schools teach one of two ways. Learn one clean sequence: which does your school teach?
19. `1rfw90` Pilot chute wrapped or in tow: emergency procedure immediately, by 2,500 ft. The AAD is not a plan.
20. `1vxd7dv` 2,500 ft: decide where you go and which side the pattern is on. 2,000 → 1,000 ft: wait over the holding point. 900 ft: start the downwind leg. 600 ft: turn onto base. 300 ft: turn onto final, into the wind. About 10 ft: flare.
21. `1xp4bxq` Never let the toggles back up: the canopy would dive. Hold, fly straight, finish near the ground and prepare for PLF.
22. `39hhji` 5-second rule: 5 s is the upper limit, not waiting. Not clearly better? Emergency procedure, and as a student never below 2,500 ft. With a spin or a canopy that obviously can't fly there are no 5 s: act now.
23. `5alreq` If there's time, loosen the chest strap and disconnect the RSL, half brakes at about 10 ft, deep breath, in the harness until you're in the water. Then cut away, swim forward out of the harness, leave the gear.
24. `6fyuro` Altitude comes before stability: unstable or spinning at 5,500 ft? Pull anyway.
25. `6q10ca` On a roof: collapse the canopy; if the wind drags you, cut away (if you can, disconnect the RSL first).
26. `9ci9yx` Obstacle below 300 ft: half brakes; the smallest flat braked turn that avoids it, or go into it with feet and knees together, flare and PLF. Never a sharp turn.
27. `csfzg` Cut away the main, at any altitude (RSL first if there's time). Then steer the reserve and land normally.
28. `jy34w3` A downplane falls fast. It's the only exception to the 1,000 ft lower limit: cut away the main regardless of altitude.
29. `kxk63` The AAD fires only very low, at a preset altitude, and the RSL is only a backup. Pulling the handles is always your job: pull the silver handle yourself, immediately.
30. `zx50gg` A biplane flies. Keep both, gently steer the front one with its rear risers, don't steer the back one, no flare, PLF. A flare can slow the front canopy and let the back one fly over it.

### Drift found and fixed
- "Hard pull" was first rendered as "trdo odpiranje" (= hard *opening*, a different thing) in `1jrdm86`, `1v5g8rl`,
  `6ct2i0`, `in6z5`, `tksgxp`; changed to "ročaj gre težko ven (hard pull)".
- `10luizh` lost its opening `<b>` tag in the first draft; restored (the tag test caught it).
- `16goig8` "C-1: first release" was first translated as "first solo jump"; corrected to "prva izpustitev
  (inštruktorja te prvič spustita)", since AFF release means the instructors let go, not a solo exit.
- `1yp3d0i` "Relax, breathe": a typo gave "diši" (= smell); fixed to "dihaj".
- Tutor prompt (`108z8a3`): "Reply in English" became "Odgovarjaj v slovenščini" on purpose, as in the other languages.
- Numbers use the Slovenian thousands dot (5.500 ft, 13.000 ft) and decimal comma (6,2 M); units kept as in English.
