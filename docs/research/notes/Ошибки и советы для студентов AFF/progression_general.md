# AFF to USPA A-license: general progression, safety habits, and "what instructors wish students knew"

Scope note: about 20 search/fetch calls. Primary sources are USPA (SIM/BSRs, A-license page, dive-flow index), Parachutist "Rating Corner" columns, and drop zone blogs (Skydive Mag / Skydive Algarve, Skydive Ramblers). Reddit threads could not be read directly: search returned only mirror snippets, so Reddit-based claims are weak and are flagged.

## 1. What is the structure (ISP Categories A–H, A-license) and the hard rules a student must know?

### Takeaway
The USPA Integrated Student Program (ISP) runs Categories A–H. AFF freefall dive flows cover A–D (C and D split into C1/C2 and D1/D2), followed by coach-led freefall and canopy dives in E–H, then the A-license Check Dive. The A license needs 25 jumps, a completed card, a check dive, and written (75%+) and oral (100%) exams. Key BSRs for students: a 14 mph wind limit on a ram-air, AAD and RSL required, 3,000 ft minimum container opening, and no jumping within 8 hours of alcohol.

### Cited Findings
- USPA's dive-flow index lists AFF freefall flows A, B, C1, C2, D1, D2. From E onward the flows are "Freefall" E1/E2, F1(/F2), G1–G3 and H, and every category also has a canopy dive flow. The A-License Check Dive comes after these. — [USPA Dive Flows](https://www.uspa.org/diveflows)
- The ISP is in SIM Section 4 and tracks what a student has completed. All students (tandem, IAD, static line, AFF) get the same A-license training. After Category C, each category adds more canopy emphasis. After the aerial-maneuver jumps with an instructor, a USPA Coach takes over group freefall training. — [Rating Corner: Using the ISP (USPA)](https://www.uspa.org/uspa-news/rating-corner-using-the-isp)
- Early AFF advancement criteria (Jen Sharp, Parachutist, July 2019):
  - Category A: start deployment within 1,000 ft of the assigned altitude. Prompting by the instructor is allowed.
  - Category B: the same, within a 500 ft window. An actual pull is not required.
  - Category C: a stable deployment with no instructor contact, with wave-off and pull at the assigned altitude and no written leeway. Sharp notes many instructors informally allow 500 ft.
  — [Rating Corner: Clarifying Goals for Early AFF Jumps](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
- BSR: students who jump with harness-hold must "reliably deploy in the belly-to-earth orientation at the correct altitude without assistance" before they jump without two AFF instructors. — [USPA SIM 2-1 BSRs](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- A-license requirements:
  - current USPA membership
  - introduction to the SIM
  - a completed A-License Progression Card
  - 25 jumps in total. Tandem, military freefall and static-line jumps can count with verified documentation.
  - the Check Dive
  - a written exam (75% or better) and an oral exam (100%)

  Privileges: jumping without supervision, packing your own main, formation skydives, and water-landing training.
  — [USPA A-License Requirements](https://www.uspa.org/members/licenses/a-license-requirements-and-forms)
- Conflict or wording drift: a drop zone blog says USPA changed the wording to "25 freefall skydives" and requires 5 group jumps. Another DZ says the card must be stamped within 60 days of completion. The current USPA page fetched says "25 jumps" and that tandem jumps can count, and it does not mention group-jump counts or a deadline. — [Skydive Tecumseh](https://www.skydivetecumseh.com/2024/06/21/skydiving-license-levels-explained/); [USPA A-License page](https://www.uspa.org/members/licenses/a-license-requirements-and-forms)
- Student wind limit (BSR H): "14 mph for ram-air canopies", and 10 mph for round reserves. Licensed jumpers have no BSR wind limit. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- Student equipment (BSR M.2): until the A license, a student rig must include:
  - a piggyback harness and container
  - a single-point riser release
  - a reserve static line (RSL)
  - a functional AAD within its service schedule
  - a rigid helmet and an altimeter
  - a ram-air main and a steerable reserve

  — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- The minimum container opening altitude for all students and A-license holders is 3,000 ft AGL. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- Alcohol and drugs (BSR F): no jumping while under the influence, or appearing to be, and no jumping "within 8 hours after the consumption of any alcoholic beverage." — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- Currency:
  - Students who haven't jumped in the preceding 30 days should make at least one jump under the direct supervision of an appropriately rated instructor.
  - For A-license holders the gap is 60 days. The supervised jump must show altitude awareness, control on all axes, tracking, and canopy skills.
  - For B it is 90 days, and for C/D it is 180 days.
  - Returning jumpers may need a larger canopy, and a layoff of a year or more needs thorough retraining.

  — [USPA SIM 4-2 Currency](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
- Drop zones may be stricter than this. Older 30/60/90-day student tiers (repeat the last dive after 60 days, and so on) are cited on forums but couldn't be found in current USPA text. Spaceland uses 30 days → a recurrency jump or the last passed dive flow. — [dropzone.com thread](https://www.dropzone.com/forums/topic/269550-student-recurrency-requirements/); [Skydive Spaceland currency](https://florida.skydivespaceland.com/skydiving-currency-use-it-or-lose-it/)

### Inferences
- Because Category C has no altitude leeway on paper, pull-time discipline (wave off and pull exactly on time) is the single most "pass/fail" item early on.
- Since the SIM recommends a supervised jump after 30 days without jumping, a student who spreads AFF over months pays for extra recurrency jumps and repeats. Jumping AFF in a tight block is cheaper and safer.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch the per-category advancement criteria for D–H. The individual pages at uspa.org/diveflows/... were not opened. The writer should link the category pages rather than list criteria from memory.
- I didn't verify whether a separate "canopy course" is now mandatory for the A license (one DZ blog mentions a canopy proficiency card requirement). The fetched USPA A-license page does not mention one.
- Cloud clearance and visibility rules were not in the BSR text fetched. They are FAA rules (FAR 105).

## 2. What are the most common reasons students repeat AFF levels?

### Takeaway
Sources point to the same failure modes: being tense or stiff, so the student can't learn or fly; losing altitude awareness or pulling late; not responding to instructor hand signals; problems finding or pulling the main handle; a poor exit presentation; and scraping through an earlier level with only bare-minimum stability. About 10 minutes of tunnel before AFF is widely said to reduce repeats. That claim comes from an instructor's opinion, not data.

### Cited Findings
- Dan Brodsky-Chenfeld (Rating Corner, March 2022) lists student problems instructors watch for:
  - drifting away from the instructor ("Students can lose it quickly")
  - not seeing or responding to hand signals
  - being stressed, scared or stiff, so not learning
  - trouble finding or pulling the main handle, which means an immediate re-dock
  - losing control at pull time, even good students
  - an unpredictable fall rate. Light, flexible students fall fast; heavier, flat ones float.

  — [Rating Corner: AFF Tips (USPA)](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-aff-tips)
- On exit, "presenting" the belly to the relative wind matters more than grabbing a grip, and the instructors, not the student, choose where the formation goes. — [Rating Corner: AFF Tips](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-aff-tips)
- Not responding to signals, not reaching for the pilot chute, and going back to a "natural body position" instead of starting deployment all fail the deployment criterion. A student who passes B with bare-minimum stability and awareness is unlikely to pass C on the first try. — [Rating Corner: Clarifying Goals for Early AFF Jumps](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
- An AFF instructor and CCI (Andy Pointer, Skydive Algarve, Dec 2020) says about 10 minutes of tunnel before the course lowers the risk of repeating levels, but it isn't essential. Tell the tunnel instructors you have AFF booked so they teach the AFF body position and turn inputs. — [Skydive Mag: 5 Hot Tips for your AFF course](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
- Pointer also says flexibility and body awareness affect AFF progress more than raw fitness, so stretch or do yoga beforehand. — [Skydive Mag](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
- Tunnel debate on forums: some say the sensory overload of a first jump wipes out tunnel skills, while others say tunnel time helps you get past that overload. Ten minutes in the tunnel is often equated to about 10 skydives of freefall time. These are forum opinions. — [dropzone.com "how much time?"](https://www.dropzone.com/forums/topic/59620-how-much-time%3F/); [dropzone.com newbie tunnel questions](https://www.dropzone.com/forums/topic/59885-very-much-a-newbie...wind-tunnel-questions/)
- USPA has a separate path. Students who train in a tunnel before a first jump with a single AFF instructor must show basic stability (neutral body position), heading control, and forward and backward movement in the tunnel. — [Rating Corner: Wind-Tunnel Training and the First Skydive](https://www.uspa.org/Information/News/rating-corner-wind-tunnel-training-and-the-first-skydive) (summarised from search results; the page itself wasn't opened)
- Skydive Ramblers (2019) says to do the basic AFF course first and then ask how tunnel time fits into the DZ's program. This is a different order from Pointer's advice to do tunnel before AFF. — [Ramblers: 7 Insider Tips](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/)
- Long gaps between jumps cost both knowledge and confidence (Ramblers). The SIM recommends a supervised jump after 30 days for students. — [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/); [USPA SIM 4-2](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
- Information overload hurts students. A Rating Corner column says early-category students should be taught only what they need for that specific jump. — [Rating Corner: Information Overload](https://parachutist.com/Article/letters-197)

### Inferences
- A repeat usually comes from a few things practised too little on the ground: the exit arch, altitude checks, the wave-off and pull sequence, and responding to hand signals. Ground rehearsal of these is the cheapest fix.
- Tunnel time mainly helps with relaxation and fall-rate or heading control. It doesn't replace practice in altitude awareness or pull discipline.

### Gaps
- I found no statistics on how often AFF levels are repeated, or which level is repeated most. Anecdotes say "one failed level" is common. One example is a YouTube course video titled "(One Failed Level)": [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QjIbC0GjBKc).
- I found no instructor-written source on chair flying, dirt dives, or saying EPs out loud specifically, although USPA dive flows exist for rehearsal. A source for these practices is still needed.

## 3. What habits keep new jumpers uninjured through their first 100 jumps, and what to review before every jump day?

### Takeaway
The best-sourced habits are:
- a gear check at four points every jump: handles, 3-ring, chest and leg straps, pins, and the AAD switched on
- respecting the student BSRs: winds, no alcohol within 8 hours, and currency
- staying current and doing recurrency after 30 days
- keeping canopy size conservative
- watching the landing pattern
- not rushing progression after AFF

Sources on hydration, fatigue, nerves, choosing a DZ, and logbooks were thin.

### Cited Findings
- USPA says every jumper's equipment should be inspected four times: before putting it on, right after putting it on, before boarding, and before exit. — [USPA SIM 4-4 (via search)](https://uspa.org/sim/4-4)
- A misrouted or unsecured chest strap or leg strap can let a jumper fall out of the harness. — [USPA Safety Check: Knowing Your Gear](https://www.uspa.org/safety-check-knowing-your-gear)
- Skydive Perris's "Check of Threes" covers:
  - three rings
  - handles seated, with free-moving cables
  - reserve pin seated and sealed by an FAA rigger
  - AAD on for the day
  - main pin and closing loop (no more than 10% wear)
  - bridle routing

  It is customary to get a gear check from another qualified jumper before boarding. — [Skydive Perris: Skydiving Gear Checks](https://skydiveperris.com/blog/skydiving-gear-checks/)
- In Category E the student gets an in-depth gear-inspection training session, ideally taught by a rigger. — [USPA SIM 4-4 (via search)](https://uspa.org/sim/4-4)
- The AAD is mandatory for students, and any AAD activation on a student jump needs an incident report within 48 hours. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
- For currency and canopy choice, USPA says "Great care is necessary when choosing canopy size". Returning jumpers may need a larger, more conservative canopy, and currency training should cover canopy-flight planning, canopy emergency procedures (CEPs), the pattern, and the flare. — [USPA SIM 4-2](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
- One forum post quotes an AFF instructor's priority order: 1) open the canopy, 2) land safely, 3) don't hurt anyone. — [dropzone.com "the consequences"](https://www.dropzone.com/forums/topic/58217-the-consequences./)
- A student's 100-jump retrospective (Black Knights, UK, May 2026) says finishing AFF doesn't mean mastery. The post-AFF period felt like starting again from one jump. — [BKPC: One year, 100 jumps](https://bkpc.co.uk/2026/05/09/one-year-100-jumps-a-shift-in-perspective/)
- Forum advice is to build skill on 2-ways and with coaches rather than drifting into unstructured solos, and not to rush tunnel or freefall progression. This is weak, older forum material. — [dropzone.com "off AFF, now what?"](https://www.dropzone.com/forums/topic/39099-off-aff,-now-what%3F/)
- A Reddit commenter in an AFF thread spent weather holds watching landing patterns. This is a mirror snippet, so it is a weak source. — [r/SkyDiving via mirror](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/SkyDiving/comments/1i72gbl/aff_at_skydive_arizona)
- Mental prep (Pointer, 2020):
  - watch a few good AFF videos, but avoid scary footage
  - clear your chores and bills before the course to cut stress
  - no heavy drinking the night before refresher training
  - wear comfortable clothes and trainers, no loose jewellery
  - no GoPro on AFF
  - check your insurance if training abroad

  — [Skydive Mag](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
- Ramblers (2019):
  - plan a whole day at the DZ for each AFF jump, because of training before and after and weather holds
  - don't watch videos out of context; trust the instructors' order of teaching
  - stay after jumps and learn from experienced jumpers

  — [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/)
- USPA's free Online Ground School covers equipment, exit, freefall, canopy flight, landing and emergency procedures, and suits refresher review. — [USPA Online Ground School](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/landing); [USPA FJC](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/fjc)
- USPA also publishes category dive flows and category quizzes to review before each jump. — [USPA Dive Flows](https://www.uspa.org/diveflows); [USPA Category Quizzes](https://www.uspa.org/quiz)

### Suggested "review before every jump day" checklist (assembled from the sources above)
1. Today's dive flow for your category, freefall and canopy. — [USPA Dive Flows](https://www.uspa.org/diveflows)
2. Emergency procedures and canopy emergencies, plus the decision altitudes your DZ uses. One FJC slide set says to act if the canopy isn't controllable by 2,500 ft. That figure is DZ-specific. — [USPA Online Ground School](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/landing); [FJC slides (SlideShare)](https://es.slideshare.net/ChristinaArango/first-jump-course)
3. Pull altitude and wave-off. Category C has no written altitude leeway. — [Rating Corner, Sharp 2019](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
4. Winds against the 14 mph student limit, and a landing-pattern plan. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
5. No alcohol in the last 8 hours. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1)
6. Days since your last jump. If more than 30, expect recurrency. — [USPA SIM 4-2](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
7. Gear check four times: handles, 3-ring, straps, pins, AAD on. — [USPA SIM 4-4](https://uspa.org/sim/4-4); [Skydive Perris](https://skydiveperris.com/blog/skydiving-gear-checks/)

### Inferences
- The habits most likely to prevent injury in the first 100 jumps are canopy-related: a conservative wing loading, pattern discipline, and a good flare. USPA's growing canopy emphasis from Category C onward reflects this.
- Saying EPs out loud and touching the handles in the hanging harness or on the ground before each jump is standard DZ practice. I found no citable source in this pass.

### Gaps
- I found no citable source on hydration, fatigue, sleep, or nerves specifically for AFF students.
- Nothing citable on choosing a dropzone or instructor (student-to-instructor ratio, rental gear, weather pattern, packages). Ramblers only recommends buying AFF packages to save money: [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/).
- No source on logbook best practice. USPA requires jumps to be documented for the license, but the specifics weren't fetched.
- No source on flare or PLF technique or landing-injury statistics for students. Another researcher may cover this.
- There is no formal post-AFF "canopy course" requirement in the sources fetched. USPA ISP canopy dive flows exist for every category.

## 4. "What instructors wish students knew" (sourced list)

1. **Relax.** A stiff, scared student can't learn or be released, and feeling safe and relaxed is what lets the instructor let go. — [Rating Corner: AFF Tips, Brodsky-Chenfeld 2022](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-aff-tips)
2. **On exit, present your belly to the wind** and let the instructors place the formation. Don't fight for grips. — [Rating Corner: AFF Tips](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-aff-tips)
3. **Watch for and respond to hand signals.** Missing them leads to a re-dock and often a repeat. — [Rating Corner: AFF Tips](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-aff-tips); [Sharp 2019](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
4. **Find and pull the main handle on time, every time.** On Category C the pull altitude has no written leeway. — [Sharp 2019](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
5. **Don't scrape through a level.** Passing B at bare minimum makes a first-try pass on C unlikely. — [Sharp 2019](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7495/Rating-Corner%20-%20Clarifying-Goals-for-Early-AFF-Jumps)
6. **Learn only what you need for today's jump.** Too much information early on works against you. — [Rating Corner: Information Overload](https://parachutist.com/Article/letters-197)
7. **Don't binge random YouTube or scary videos.** Trust your instructors' order of teaching. — [Ramblers 2019](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/); [Pointer, Skydive Mag 2020](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
8. **Jump regularly.** Gaps cost skills and confidence, and more than 30 days means recurrency. — [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/); [USPA SIM 4-2](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
9. **Flexibility beats raw fitness.** Stretch, and try a little tunnel time if you can. Tell the tunnel coach you're doing AFF. — [Skydive Mag 2020](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
10. **Don't show up hungover.** The rule is 8 hours bottle-to-jump, and instructors notice. — [USPA SIM 2-1](https://www.uspa.org/SIM/2-1); [Skydive Mag](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/5-hot-tips-for-your-aff-course/)
11. **Block out the whole day** for training before and after each jump and for weather holds. — [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.com.au/blog/7-insider-tips-to-help-you-succeed-through-aff/)
12. **Getting the A license is the start, not the finish.** Keep jumps 26–100 structured, conservative and coached. — [BKPC 2026](https://bkpc.co.uk/2026/05/09/one-year-100-jumps-a-shift-in-perspective/); [USPA SIM 4-2 on canopy size](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-2)
