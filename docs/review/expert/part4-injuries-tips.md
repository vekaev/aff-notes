# Expert review: part4-injuries-tips   (2026-11-16, `src/content/part4.ts` lines 941–1334 chapter `real` "How people actually get hurt", and 1489–2056 chapter `tips` "Common mistakes and tips, stage by stage")

Not covered here: chapter `mistakes` ("What went wrong: real jumps", the video clips, lines 1337–1488). I treated it as a separate video scope.

## Summary
I mostly trust this part. Nearly every statistic matches its source: Westman 2007, Fer 2021, Ellitsgaard 1987, Damhuis 2024, Forrester 2023, the USPA 2020 fatality summary and 2024 non-fatal summary, the CSPA 2025 report, and the Bell, Planche Wallace and Schock articles. The USPA rules are right too: Cat A–C pull criteria, BSR wind and alcohol, the A-license, 30-day currency, lost instructors, altitude every 3 s, decision altitude and hard deck.

The serious problems are landing technique, not numbers:
1. "**legs up**" is given as landing advice for the student's own solo jumps. That is a tandem-passenger instruction. A solo student lands feet first with feet and knees together.
2. "**Full flare at ~10 ft, hold**" can be read as "finish the flare at 10 ft". USPA says start at about twice your height and finish 1–3 ft off the ground.
3. "**Two tries**, then cut away" for a spinning canopy is not the time rule the app teaches elsewhere (5 s).
4. "Category A has two goals… everything else is a bonus" plays down the canopy and landing criteria, which is where students get hurt.

## Missing
- **What lifting the legs means.** The page needs one clear line: solo landing = feet and knees together, feet first, PLF (USPA ISP Cat A, Landing). Right now the only leg instruction is "legs up" (#1).
- **Downwind landing.** USPA Cat A first-jump criteria ask the student to "describe how to handle a downwind landing". The injury chapter's "Downwind landing and turbulence" card says only "Land into the wind". It should add: if you are low and not facing into the wind, **don't turn**: land straight with a level wing, flare and PLF (SIM 4-1 landing priorities: wing level, clear area, flare, PLF). The low-turn card hints at this ("Level wing first, then target and wind"), but the downwind card itself doesn't.
- **Footwear.** In the Dutch study, 75% of injured jumpers wore low sports shoes (Damhuis 2024). One line ("wear shoes that support the ankle, if your school allows") fits this chapter. Optional.
