# Expert review: Part III "Under canopy"   (2026-11-16; src/content/part3.ts, all chapters: deploy, pattern + turn limits + low turn, obstacles, flare + PLF, ground-school test Q26–32; src/widgets.tsx: CanopyCheck, Pattern, LowTurn)

## Summary
This part is mostly sound. The pattern numbers (2,000 holding, 900 / 600 / 300, flare at about twice your height), the
"flared too high: stop and hold" rule, the obstacle procedures, "level wing first" and "never save the gear" all match
the current SIM 4-1 and the ISP Category A. The worst problems:
1. The post-pull count: the app says count to 5 and "don't judge it earlier". The USPA AFF syllabus counts to 3,000,
   then checks the pilot chute and canopy.
2. The flare look: the app says "eyes on the horizon". USPA says look forward and down at about 45°. The quiz marks
   the horizon as the right answer.
3. Water landing is missing three SIM steps: loosen the chest strap, disconnect the RSL, and cut away once in the water.
4. Low-turn recovery (SIM 4-1 F) is missing.
5. Q27's answer (300 ft) probably isn't the school's key, since Q28 already asks about 300 ft.

## Missing
- **Landing priorities in this part** (SIM 4-1 B): 1 level wing flying straight, 2 a clear open area (even farther away), 3 flare and PLF. They are only in Part IV (part4.ts:1812). One line in the flare or low-turn chapter would tie the chapter together.
- **Left hand balances the reach** (ISP Cat A): "keep your left hand overhead for balance while reaching with your right", then return to a neutral arch. Step 1 (:28) mentions only the right hand.
- **Handle not found / hard pull** (ISP Cat A): no more than two tries or 5 seconds, then the reserve. Probably in Part IV; a link from the deploy step would help.
- **Flare finish height** (ISP Cat A): arms fully down when the feet are about 1–3 ft above the ground. The app says "finished at touchdown", which is close but less precise.
- **Trees** (SIM 4-1 G): prepare to PLF, because you often pass through the tree to the ground. Cover the face with hands, elbows tight to the chest.
- **Water after entry** (SIM 4-1 G): a container with a packed reserve can float for a while, but watch for the main lines. Swim upwind or upstream, away from the lines.

## Checked and fine
About 70 claims are correct. They include: pull at 5,500 ft (ISP Cat A/B); there / square / stable / steerable and the
practice flare; check altitude and traffic after the check; decision altitude 2,500 ft for students (SIM 4-1); over the
holding area by 2,000 ft and choosing an alternate field by 2,000 ft (SIM 4-1 G, ISP A); pattern 900 / 600 / 300
(ISP A); flare at about 10 ft; flared too high: hold, never let up (ISP A); trees: half brakes, feet together, middle of
the tree, grab the trunk, don't climb down (SIM 4-1 G); water: half brakes at 10 ft, deep breath, stay in the harness
until in the water; buildings: flat turn along the wall, flare about 10 ft above the first point of contact, PLF; power
lines: parallel, feet and knees together, head turned, nobody touches you; never save the gear; land straight rather
than turn low; turn limits labelled a DZ rule; PLF on the side, chin to chest. All four hazards are the SIM 4-1 G list.
The pattern map geometry (wind, windsock, holding area upwind, legs) and the low-turn side view are correct. Hero
numbers match Part III. The ES, UK and RU versions of the safety lines keep the meaning (except #18). Sources:
uspa.org/sim/4-1 (Skydiving Emergencies, sections B, F, G), uspa.org/skydiveschool/A (ISP Category A), the ISP
Category B search result (AFF deployment 5,500 ft, 900 ft pattern entry), USPA Safety Check, and the YouTube titles and
descriptions of the six credited clips.
