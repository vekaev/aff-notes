# Expert review: part3-canopy   (2026-11-16; `src/content/part3.ts` chapters deploy, pattern, obstacles, flare/PLF, test; plus the canopy numbers in `src/components/Hero.tsx` and the `Pattern` / `LowTurn` drawings in `src/widgets.tsx`)

Sources opened: USPA SIM 4-1 (uspa.org/sim/4-1: sections E Deployment Emergencies, Low Turns, G Obstacle landings, Landing Priorities, Canopy Right-of-Way); USPA ISP Category A (uspa.org/skydiveschool/a: Main Deployment, Canopy Check, Canopy Flight, Landing Pattern, Final Approach and Landing, PLF, Obstacle Landing table, Cat A dive flows); YouTube oEmbed titles for every credited clip, plus frames from `public/media/x_lowturn2.mp4` and `flare_cue.mp4`.

## Summary
Mostly trustworthy. The pattern numbers (900 / 600 / 300, holding area by 2,000, alternate field by 2,000, flare at about 10 ft), the landing priorities and most obstacle steps match the SIM and ISP. The worst problems are in the obstacle cards. **Water** leaves out the SIM's before-entry steps (loosen the chest strap, disconnect the RSL, flotation) and the cutaway once you are in the water. **Power lines** leave out "drop handles, slow down" and "feet on the ground: disconnect RSL and cut away". The low-turn box says "go into" a fence instead of the SIM's flat braked turn to keep a level wing, and the page never teaches the SIM low-turn recovery. Two more things need fixing: "decide by 2,500" should say decide **and act** by 2,500, and "eyes on the horizon" differs from the ISP's "forward and down at 45°" for judging flare height. The 5-second count and some sequences differ from USPA's AFF text (3-count, flare first in the control check). Those are for the instructors to settle.

## Missing
- **Canopy right-of-way**, in the pattern chapter: "Look before you turn. Turn right to avoid other jumpers. The lower jumper has the right-of-way." (SIM 4-1 Survival Skills; ISP Cat A Canopy Flight: about to collide, both steer right.) Part IV has only "Collision ahead: both turn right"; the pattern chapter, where the traffic is, has nothing.
- **Right after opening**: the SIM says to keep your eyes forward for jumpers who opened nearby and to steer with rear risers if needed before the canopy check (SIM 4-1 "Canopy Collisions"). Most collisions happen right after opening or below 1,000 ft.
- **The pattern-entry fallback**: if you can't reach the 900-ft point, fly to the 600-ft or 300-ft point you can reach in time, and if you can't reach any, land in an open area. The best alternate may be behind you (ISP Cat A Canopy Flight).
- **Dragged by the canopy after landing**: pull in one toggle and its steering line; cut away only as a last resort or if injured (ISP Cat A, "Recovering the Canopy in Higher Winds").
- **Stand-up vs PLF**: the ISP says stand up only after a soft touchdown you are sure you can stay on your feet for; otherwise PLF. The app's "Be ready for one on every landing" is right, but nowhere does it say when standing up is allowed.
- **Water temperature**: the SIM says water below 70 °F sharply limits survival time, and floating may be better than swimming (SIM 4-1 G). This matters near Tracy (the California Aqueduct and the Delta).

## Checked and fine
About 70 claims checked and correct. They include: 900/600/300 pattern legs and turning at the altitude or the landmark; holding area reached at or above 2,000 ft; alternate field chosen by 2,000 ft; 2,500 ft student decision altitude; landing priorities (level wing, then clear area, then flare and PLF); landing straight even downwind; flare at about 10 ft (12–15 ft in the Rhythm clip, which the caption says correctly); "stop and hold, never let the toggles back up" when flaring high; tree, building-wall and power-line-parallel steps; flare 10 ft above first contact on a building; never recover gear; the PLF five points (balls of the feet, calf, thigh, buttock, lat), chin to chest, feet and knees together, knees slightly bent; 4 hazards; the right answers and distractors of all 17 quiz questions other than #7 and #9; the drawing (wind from the right, right-hand pattern, final into the wind, holding point upwind of the target); and the first-screen numbers 2,500 / 2,000 / 1,000 / 900·600·300 / 10 ft, which agree with the chapter. The 7 credited videos match their titles and credits (oEmbed). The safety-critical lines in ES, UK and RU say the same as EN; none of them changes the meaning. Main sources: USPA SIM 4-1 (uspa.org/sim/4-1), USPA ISP Category A (uspa.org/skydiveschool/a).
