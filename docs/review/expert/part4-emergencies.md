Sources opened: USPA SIM 4-1 "Skydiving Emergencies" (uspa.org/sim/4-1: B Prevention, E Deployment Emergencies: decision altitudes, Total Malfunction, Partial Malfunction, Two Canopies Out); USPA ISP Category A (uspa.org/skydiveschool/a: Emergency Procedures, Canopy Problems, Two Canopies Out, RSL/AAD) and Category B (uspa.org/skydiveschool/b: deployment problems, critical altitudes); USPA Malfunction Videos page (uspa.org/vrvideos, VR series #1–#12 and their written solutions); Parachutist, July 2019: Ron Bell "Don't Delay, Cut Away! Every credited YouTube clip: title, channel, description, chapters (yt-dlp), auto-transcripts for the Bodyflight Spotlight EP video and APF "Cutaway!" Ch 2, 5 and 6, and frames from `public/media/pov_*`, `m_*`, `two_*`, `ep*`.

## Summary
The backbone is right and matches USPA: decide and act by 2,500 ft, 1,000 ft hard deck, downplane as the only exception, "two tries or 5 s" for pull problems, PC-in-tow has two accepted procedures, line-over / bag lock / streamer / horseshoe = emergency procedure, tangled canopies = don't cut away. The serious problems are about **spins and time**. (1) The line-twist card is in the "fixable" family and shows a first-person clip that USPA itself titles **"Spinning Line Twists"** and answers with "cut away and pull the reserve". The card tells the student to kick first. (2) "Cut away if the spin **gets faster**" and the 5-second quiz ("how long do you take to decide? About 5 seconds") both teach waiting, when USPA, APF and Parachutist all say a spin or a high-speed malfunction means **act now**. (3) Biplane says "small or no flare", but USPA says **no flare**. Also: the two-hand method is credited to "USPA's safety director", and the source doesn't say that. The AAD reason given is wrong. The RSL is never explained. One of the five two-canopy configurations is missing.

## Missing
- **RSL / MARD**: what it does, that the student still pulls silver, and that it should be disconnected (if possible) in two-out situations before a cutaway. Sources: ISP Cat A (RSL definition, "always your job to pull the handles"), SIM 4-1 E Two Canopies Out. The app mentions the RSL only in the Part I gear check and Part III obstacles.
- **Second canopy still deploying**: trap it between your legs (SIM 4-1 E). See #13.
- **Spinning vs calm line twists**: the single most important split in this chapter. See #1.
- **One toggle came unstowed on opening, the canopy turns**: release the other toggle (or two full flares). Sources: USPA VR #11 description and APF Ch 2 "Premature brake release". Today it is only in Tips (l.1695). It belongs in the "fixable" family, because a student who sees a turn should not go straight to EP.
- **Pilot-chute hesitation** (twist and look over the shoulder, ISP Cat A/B), **hard-pull technique** (elbow against the container, ISP Cat B) and **lost handle** (sweep down the container side, ISP Cat B): these are in Tips (l.1671-1695), not here. Link the "Hard pull" card to them.
- **Altitude before cutaway**: see #5.
