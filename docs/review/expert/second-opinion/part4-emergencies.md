# Expert review: Part IV "When it goes wrong"   (2026-11-16; `src/content/part4.ts` all chapters: ep, mal, two, real, mistakes, tips, questions; cross-checked `src/components/Hero.tsx`, `part3.ts`, `dropzone.ts`)

## Summary
The core emergency numbers are right and agree everywhere: students decide and act by 2,500 ft, the cutaway hard deck is 1,000 ft, a downplane is cut away at any altitude, pilot chute in tow has two accepted procedures, and lost or hard pull means two tries or 5 s. These match SIM 4-1 and ISP Category A/B. The 5-second rule, the "more than 4,000 cutaways a year" figure and the case studies all trace to real Parachutist articles. The worst problems are in **two canopies out**. The biplane card says "small or no flare" (USPA: don't flare) and doesn't say to steer with the **rear risers, toggles stowed**. The side-by-side card says "steer the bigger one" (USPA: the one more directly overhead, on rear risers) and states "don't cut away" as fact, while SIM 4-1 allows cutting away. Method B of the EP is credited to "USPA's safety director", but he never recommends it. Its "both hands on silver" step doesn't match the clip shown beside it. The AAD explanation gives the wrong reason the AAD won't help. The "7% low turns" figure is quoted against the wrong total.

## Missing
- **Two canopies, one inflated and one still deploying** (the most likely student two-out after an AAD fire or a pilot chute in tow on a "reserve only" EP): SIM 4-1 E says contain the deploying canopy between your legs, or if it can't be stopped, let it inflate and assess. The page doesn't cover it. Add a card.
- **Pilot chute in tow, reserve-only option:** SIM 4-1 E warns that the main may then open, so be ready for a two-out. The card (:511) presents both options without that warning.
- **Altitude check inside the EP:** ISP Cat A frames the cutaway as "check your altitude… above your cutaway hard deck of 1,000 feet, then cut away and pull your reserve". Neither sequence A nor B starts with a glance at the altimeter. Add the glance, or a one-line reminder above the steps.
- **Broken steering line:** the USPA malfunction video series (Skydive Mag, Nov 2021) says you can steer and flare on the rear risers instead of cutting away. That is a common, landable problem students meet. Add it to the fixable group: "rear risers to steer and flare; ask your instructors".
- **Questions for the instructors** (:2058 list): add "Biplane: rear risers with toggles stowed?", "RSL or Skyhook in a two-out: disconnect or not?" and "After pulling silver: right-shoulder look?".
- **Slider stuck (the plan's open item):** the evidence is mixed. ISP Cat A says a canopy-control check (flare) solves most minor problems. Parachutist "Keep an Eye Out: Hung Slider" says flare and hold until the canopy slows. Some schools pump the rear risers. The card's hedge is acceptable. Better: "Flare and hold a few seconds, or pump (toggles or rear risers, as your school teaches). Not fixed by 2,500: EP."
