# Expert review: Part II "Freefall"   (2026-11-16; `src/content/part2.ts` all chapters: body, flow, altitude, signals; `src/widgets.tsx` Altimeter; cross-checked numbers in `src/components/Hero.tsx`, `part1/3/4.ts`, `dropzone.ts`)

## Summary
Part II is in good shape where it matters most. The pull altitude (5,500), lock on (6,000), altitude checks every 3 s, "lost one instructor, carry on; lost both, pull now", the neutral leg position and the practice touches all match the USPA SIM Category A page. The numbers agree with the first screen, Part III and the dropzone chapter. The worst problems: (1) the lock-on card explains the 1,000 ft wrongly (6,000 to 5,500 is only 500 ft; USPA's 1,000 ft is the wave-off-and-pull sequence that *starts* at 5,500); (2) the "overloaded" default says "pull at 5,500" without "or now if you're already lower", and Part II never says "pull at your altitude, stable or not"; (3) the wave-off is hedged "if your school teaches it" although it is the USPA AFF standard, and the page never says what a wave-off is; (4) the circle-of-awareness order differs from USPA and from the app's own clip caption; (5) the coloured arcs on the altimeter drawing don't match a real student altimeter.

## Missing
- **Signals the student will see at the pull** (uspa.org/skydiveschool/A, hand-signal list): **Open hand = let go of the pilot chute** (students sometimes hold the PC after throwing) and **Legs in = bring the legs in slightly**. Both are in USPA's Category A list and not in the 12 cards.
- **Pull priorities and "pull anyway"**: see finding 2.
- **How long freefall lasts**: 13,000 to 5,500 is about 45–50 s (≈10 s to reach terminal, then ~1,000 ft per 5.5–6 s at ~120 mph). From lock on to pull is only ~3 s. That shows why the dive flow must be done by about 10,000, and why lock on is a hard stop. (Physics from terminal velocity. The USPA Cat A page gives no time figure.)
- **What the wave-off is and why** (both arms overhead: "I know my altitude and I'm pulling"): see finding 4.
- **Unstable after exit**: check altitude, arch until the horizon is level, follow signals (USPA Cat A, "Instability"). The flow covers losing instructors but not instability.
