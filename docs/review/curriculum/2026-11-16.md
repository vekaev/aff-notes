# Curriculum review: coverage, order, learning (2026-11-16)

Reviewer: AFF-instructor / learning-design pass (`.claude/agents/aff-curriculum-reviewer.md`). Read the whole app once in
order (Hero, Parts I–V, all quizzes) as a student before jump 1, then compared with USPA material. Proposes only; nothing
in `src/` changed.

**Overall.** Strong on the things the owner was taught in class: climb-out, exit count, dive flow, signals, pattern,
flare/PLF, EP, malfunction families, two-out, obstacle landings. Real clips and decision-style quiz items are already
better than most DZ handouts. The holes are the parts of a jump the ground course mentions in one sentence and students
forget: **the ground before the plane, freefall problems other than a bad canopy, traffic and collisions under canopy,
recovering from a low turn, landing off and what happens after touchdown, and the road after category A.** The second
issue is structure: emergencies and "learn from others" share one part, so the emergency answer is mixed with 4 bar
charts and 13 incident clips.

## New sections

### 1. Part 0 "On the ground" (new part before Part I; move "Your gear" into it)
Real order of a jump day: briefing → wind → gear → checks → walk to the plane. Students get hurt on the ground by props
and by a gear fault nobody caught.
- **Your briefing**: the instructor goes through exit, freefall, EP review, canopy plan and landing pattern, and the DZ's
  hazards **on an aerial photo or map** (SIM 4-4 B "briefs students"). Ask to point at the holding point and the
  alternate fields yourself.
- **Wind before you board**: windsock → which way final points → left- or right-hand pattern. Student limit 14 mph for
  ram-air (BSR 2-1 H.1.1); the school decides, but you should know why a load is on hold.
- **Gear checklist, same order every time** (SIM 4-4 C), as a tick-list with photos: helmet and chin strap, goggles,
  altimeter reads zero and doesn't cover a handle, three rings + RSL, main pin seated and closing loop not worn, pilot
  chute in its pouch, chest/leg straps threaded and snug, reserve pin + seal + data card in date (14 CFR 105.43, AC 105-2E),
  AAD on, shoes with no hooks, pockets empty.
- **Walking to the plane**: approach fixed-wing aircraft from the rear, never through the prop arc (USPA Cat A quiz Q1);
  hand over your handles and pilot chute.
- **Boarding and seatbelts**: belts for taxi, takeoff and landing (FAR 91.107 per Cat A quiz Q2–3); DZ belt-off altitude
  (SIM 4-4: 1,500 ft AGL or DZ policy) — then keep the current "until the instructor says" rule as the student version.
- Keep the existing "Know your rig" drawing and the quiz here.

### 2. Part II, new chapter "When freefall goes wrong" (after "When to check altitude", before "Hand signals")
- **Pull priorities** (SIM 4-1 B): 1 pull, 2 pull at your altitude, 3 pull stable. "Always prioritize altitude over
  stability" (Cat A page). Unstable at 5,500? Pull.
- **Lost both instructors: pull now** (Cat A page). Lost one: carry on. (Move the red callout from the dive flow here and
  link it.)
- **Can't find the handle / hard pull**: two tries or 5 s, then the reserve (SIM 4-1 E total malfunction: "When no main
  pilot chute deploys … deploy the reserve"). The app's "emergency procedure" — say which: reserve only, or full EP?
  `<q-check/>`.
- **Premature container opening** (main bag out, pilot chute still in its pouch; SIM 4-1 E): try to throw the pilot chute,
  two tries or 5 s; not found → cut away and reserve; thrown → be ready for the EP. Prevention: pin check, rig off the door
  frame.
- **Pilot-chute hesitation**: twist and look over the right shoulder (from tips, belongs here).
- **The AAD is not a plan**: fires around 750 ft (model/mode), may give you two canopies → see Two canopies out (SIM 4-3 G).

### 3. Part III, top of the part: "Landing priorities" (one card, before "Pull, count, check")
- 1 Land with a level wing, flying straight. 2 Land in a clear, open area. 3 Flare, ready to PLF (SIM 4-1 B; Cat A quiz).
- "A level wing beats a clear area; a clear area beats the target."
- Link each priority to its chapter (low turn, obstacles, flare/PLF). Every later rule then reads as an application.

### 4. Part III, inside "Flight plan and landing pattern": "Traffic and collisions"
- After opening: look forward, then all around; fly away from the jump-run line until you've seen the others (SIM 4-1 F).
- Rules of the road: look before you turn; turn right to avoid; the lower jumper has right of way (SIM 4-1 B).
- Head-on: both turn right (SIM 4-1 F).
- Collision inevitable: left arm across your handles, face away, legs spread (SIM 4-1 F).
- Most collisions happen right after opening or below 1,000 ft in the pattern (SIM 4-1 F) — fly the same pattern as
  everyone (already in the incident chapter; link it).

### 5. Part III, extend "What a low turn is": "Caught in a low turn? Recover"
- Stop the turn: pull the **opposite** toggle to a braked position; get the canopy back overhead (SIM 4-1 F).
- Flare from that braked position; expect a quick, flat flare from the extra speed.
- PLF; if you touch early, **keep flying the canopy** (SIM 4-1 F).
- Prevention stays as is; add "a braked turn is the low-altitude turn" (SIM 4-1 G trees/buildings).

### 6. Part III, after "Landing on an obstacle": "Landing off, and after you land"
- Decide **by 2,000 ft** (already there); choose the field with the longest run, check it for poles/towers (easier to
  see than wires), fences run in straight lines, trees make turbulence downwind (SIM 4-1 G; Cat C quiz).
- Into the wind if you can, **never at the cost of a low turn** (SIM 4-1 G). PLF — off-field ground is uneven.
- On the ground: look up for traffic, stand up, collapse the canopy, get off the landing area. Strong wind dragging you:
  pull one toggle in hand over hand; can't → cut away (RSL first) `<q-check/>` (SIM 4-1 G "pull one toggle in or cut
  away" in water; roof: cut away).
- Landed off: you're fine → signal/phone the DZ, gather the canopy, don't cross crops, leave gates as found, don't
  disturb livestock (SIM 4-1 G). Hurt → stay put, signal for help `<q-check/>` (DZ procedure).
- Landed under the reserve: bring the reserve back, tell your instructor; the cutaway main is searched for by others.

### 7. Part IV, EP chapter: "After you pull silver"
- Reserve pilot chute can hang in your burble: **look over the right shoulder / twist up to the right** (SIM 4-1 E).
- Reserve toggles are held by Velcro: **peel first**, then unstow (SIM 4-1 E).
- What the RSL does (pulls the reserve pin when the main risers leave; mandatory for students, SIM 4-3 F) and why you
  still pull silver yourself. Today "RSL" appears only in the checklist and the roof bullet without explanation.

### 8. Part IV, new chapter "Radio" (or a block in the flare chapter)
- The radio is a reminder, not your pilot: "Every student must learn to fly and land a parachute without assistance of a
  radio" (Parachutist, Running the Radio).
- Radio check on the ground before boarding; how you acknowledge calls (leg wiggle / toggle) `<q-check/>`.
- No calls or garbled calls: fly the plan you briefed — holding point, 1,000 ft, 900/600/300, flare at ~10 ft.
- A call that would make you turn low: level wing wins (SIM 4-1 B priorities).

### 9. Part V (general, before the Skydive California example): "After category A: B to H and your A license"
- What each category adds (USPA Skydive School / quizzes): B – turns, relative wind, gear and AAD; C – first release,
  recovery from instability, turbulence, wing loading; D – turns, spotting, cloud clearance; E – stalls, flips, packing,
  RSL; F – tracking, braked turns, student wind limit; G – group exits, collisions, gear care; H – front risers, water,
  aircraft rules. One line each, with the category's quiz link.
- A license: 25 freefall jumps, the progression card, written + oral + practical exams (SIM 3-1).
- Student rules you live by until then: 14 mph ram-air (BSR 2-1 H.1.1), jumps between sunrise and sunset (G.9), no alcohol
  8 h before (F.2), rigid helmet, altimeter, RSL and AAD (M.2).
- Gap of 30 days or more: one supervised jump after a refresher (SIM 4-2 A).
- Move "Getting through AFF and the A-license" out of the tips chapter into here.

### 10. Header + Part IV top: "Emergency card" (one screen, printable, also offline)
- Altitudes: pull 5,500 · decide by 2,500 · no cutaway below 1,000 · down-plane = cut away any altitude.
- EP in the school's version (A or B), one line.
- Nothing out → reserve · out but not flyable → EP · fixable → two tries or 5 s.
- Two out: biplane / side by side / downplane / entangled, one action each.
- Obstacles: trees, water, buildings, power lines, one line each. Landing priorities.
- A red "EP" pill in the header on every screen; a print stylesheet so it fits one A6 card.

## New test questions

Format: question · options (correct marked **bold**) · why · source. All are "scenario → what do you do".

**Ground and plane**

1. *Engines are running and you're walking out to the plane. How do you approach it?*
   **From the rear, away from the prop** · From the front so the pilot sees you · Duck low under the prop.
   Why: a turning prop is invisible. — USPA Cat A quiz Q1.
2. *Climbing to the door, your rig scrapes the door frame. What now?*
   Nothing, it's normal · **Tell the instructor and get a pin check before exit** · Hold your reserve handle.
   Why: a dislodged pin causes a premature opening. — SIM 4-1 E (prevention).
3. *The windsock is stiff and the DZ says 18 mph. You're a solo student. What happens?*
   You jump with a bigger canopy · **No jump: the student limit is 14 mph** · You jump if the instructor agrees.
   Why: BSR, not opinion. — BSR 2-1 H.1.1.

**Freefall**

4. *5,500 ft and you're slowly spinning and slightly head-low. What do you do?*
   Get stable first, then pull · **Pull now** · Signal the instructor and wait.
   Why: altitude over stability. — SIM 4-1 B pull priorities; USPA Cat C quiz Q3.
5. *6,000 ft and you're in the middle of your second circle of awareness. What now?*
   Finish the circle, then lock on · **Stop, lock on the altimeter, pull at 5,500** · One more practice touch.
   — Cat A dive flow (uspa.org/skydiveschool/A).
6. *In freefall you feel the main bag leave your back, but your hand finds the pilot chute still in its pouch. What do
   you do?* Pull silver at once · **Try to throw the pilot chute, two tries or 5 s; then cut away and reserve** · Wait for
   the AAD.
   — SIM 4-1 E, premature main-container opening.
7. *Second try at a hard pull; the handle won't move. What next?*
   Third try with both hands · **Deploy the reserve** (your school may teach the full EP `<q-check/>`) · Cut away first,
   then wait.
   — SIM 4-1 E total malfunction ("no more than two attempts or five seconds… deploy the reserve").

**Under canopy**

8. *Another canopy is coming straight at you at the same height. Which way?*
   Left · **Right — and so do they** · Pull both toggles and stop.
   — SIM 4-1 B/F.
9. *A collision is about to happen and you can't avoid it. What do you do with your body?*
   Grab their lines · **Left arm across your handles, face away, legs spread** · Cut away now.
   — SIM 4-1 F.
10. *Rank the landing priorities.*
    Target, into the wind, flare · **Level wing, clear area, flare + PLF** · Clear area, level wing, stand up.
    — SIM 4-1 B; USPA Cat A quiz Q9.
11. *At 150 ft you realise you've started a turn that's too low. What now?*
    Finish it fast to be level sooner · **Opposite toggle to a braked position, flare from there, PLF** · Let both
    toggles up for speed.
    — SIM 4-1 F low-turn recovery.
12. *2,000 ft, you won't reach the landing area. The best field has trees on its downwind side and a road with poles. Where
    do you land?*
    Right behind the tree line, out of the wind · **Upwind part of the field, away from the poles, longest straight run** ·
    Along the road, it's flat.
    Why: turbulence behind trees; wires follow roads. — SIM 4-1 G landing out; Cat C quiz Q8.
13. *Your radio has said nothing since opening. What do you do?*
    Circle the holding point until it speaks · **Fly the briefed plan: 1,000 → 900 / 600 / 300, flare at ~10 ft** · Land
    off so the instructor can find you.
    — Parachutist, Running the Radio.
14. *You just pulled silver; nothing seems to happen for a second. What helps?*
    Pull silver again · **Look over your right shoulder / twist up to the right** · Arch harder and wait.
    — SIM 4-1 E reserve activation.

**After landing**

15. *You land in a farmer's field, unhurt. What do you do?*
    Leave the canopy, walk back through the crops · **Gather your gear, leave gates as found, avoid crops and animals, tell
    the DZ** · Wait there until someone comes.
    — SIM 4-1 G.
16. *You haven't jumped for 5 weeks. What is your next jump?*
    Your next category as planned · **A refresher and a jump under direct instructor supervision** · A tandem.
    — SIM 4-2 A (30 days).

## Reorder / cut

**Order of a real jump.** Proposed parts:

0. On the ground (new): briefing · wind · Your gear (moved) · walk to the plane
I. In the plane: ride up + aircraft emergency · climb-out · exit count
II. Freefall: body · dive flow · altitude · **when freefall goes wrong** (new) · hand signals
III. Under canopy: **landing priorities** (new card) · pull, count, check · pattern + traffic · obstacles · **landing off,
   after you land** (new) · flare + PLF · landing questions
IV. Emergencies: emergency card (new) · EP (+ after you pull silver) · what can go wrong · two out · radio · ask the
   instructors
V. Learn from others (split from IV): how people get hurt · what went wrong: real jumps · mistakes and tips
VI. After category A + your dropzone + read next

Why: today Part I starts in the plane, so gear checks come after the climb; Part IV mixes "do this now" with statistics
and 13 clips, which slows the student who opens it looking for an answer.

**Smaller moves**
- "Flare" before "Obstacles": the flare is every landing; obstacles are the exception. Obstacle and landing-off chapters
  then sit together.
- "Lost both instructors: pull now" lives in the dive flow callout and in tips: move to the new freefall-problems
  chapter, link from the 13,000 row.
- At the 5,500 row of the altimeter widget, link "Can't find it / hard pull?" to the freefall-problems chapter (now the
  reader has to guess it's in Part IV under canopy malfunctions).
- "Ask the instructors" → end of the app (or a floating "my questions" list), not inside emergencies.
- "Getting through AFF and the A-license" tips → the new After-category-A chapter.

**Cut or fold away** (behind a "more" toggle, not deleted)
- Injury chapter: keep "when" (phase) and "inside the leg"; fold "which part of the body" and "what went wrong at landing"
  and the 12-link source line. The message is one sentence: the last 10 ft on the first jumps.
- "Two different 2,500 ft" callout appears in pattern and EP: keep one shared block, link from the other.
- "About 5, 4, 3, 2, 1" is explained in a callout, a quiz, two tips and the FAQ: one callout is enough.
- Hand-signal "longer version" and duplicate watch links: one link per topic.
- "Three real cases" and the Reddit callout: fold under the stats.
- Category notes (B: 90° turns, C: first release…) in the Skydive California example duplicate the new general chapter.
