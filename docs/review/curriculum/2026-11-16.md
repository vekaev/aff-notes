# Curriculum review: the app as a whole course (2026-11-16)

Reviewer: `aff-curriculum-reviewer` (AFF instructor + learning designer). I read every chapter once, in order, as a
student before a first jump (English text of `src/content/*.ts`, `Hero.tsx`, the widgets), and checked it against USPA
SIM 4-1, the BSRs (SIM 2-1), the ISP category pages (A–G) and Parachutist / USPA Safety Check columns. This report is
about **coverage, order and learning**. Line-by-line facts are in `docs/review/expert/`; where a point here overlaps
one of theirs I say so and don't repeat the detail.

**Short verdict.** The core first-jump material (exit, freefall, pull, canopy check, pattern, flare, EP,
malfunctions) is all here, in jump order, and in better shape than most DZ handouts. What's missing is mostly what
happens **between** the textbook steps: winds and ground speed, other canopies in the air, landing somewhere other
than the target, what to do after you land, and the gear-up / boarding routine. The quizzes test too much recall
("how many touches?") and too few decisions ("you're at 2,000 ft and not moving over the ground; what now?").
Part IV mixes "act now" chapters with "read at home" chapters, so the emergency answer is harder to find than it
should be.

---

## Top 10

Ranked by how much each would help a student land safely. Effort: S = under an hour of content, M = a chapter,
L = new widget or mode.

1. **New chapter: "landing somewhere else" + "after you land".** Off-DZ landings were 31% of student landing incidents
   in the Swedish data (`injury-data.md`). Right now the app gives one line ("pick a clear field by 2,000 ft"). Students
   need the whole procedure: choose the field, check it every 500 ft, fly a normal pattern there, then what to do on the
   ground. **M**
2. **New chapter: "other canopies".** Look before you turn, turn right, the lower jumper has right of way, what to do
   if a collision can't be avoided. Most canopy collisions happen below 1,000 ft in the pattern (SIM 4-1 F). Today
   this is one bullet in the tips and one incident card. **M**
3. **New chapter: "wind for students".** The 14 mph student limit, reading the windsock, ground speed (holding,
   running, crabbing), turbulence downwind of obstacles, high-wind landings and being dragged. The pattern and holding
   point only make sense once you understand wind. ISP Cat B/C teach it; the app assumes it. **M**
4. **"Four survival skills" card as the spine of the app** (altitude, pull priorities, rules of the air, landing
   priorities; Bell, USPA 2021), shown on the first screen and printable. One card a scared student can recall in the
   door or under a bad canopy. **S**
5. **Rewrite the quizzes toward decisions.** Replace about 12 recall questions with "scenario → what do you do?",
   and add a mixed 10-question "before you board" test drawn from every part. **M**
6. **Put the "act now" rules where they're needed:** "pull even if you're unstable" in the pull chapter (it's only in
   an incident card today), low-turn recovery in the low-turn section (also expert part III #5), landing priorities
   in the flare chapter. **S**
7. **EP touch-drill mode:** a timed, eyes-closed, say-it-out-loud drill for the cutaway / reserve sequence and the
   dive flow, with the hanging-harness routine from the tips. Reading isn't rehearsal. **L**
8. **Gear-up and boarding chapter, and gear before the plane.** SHAGG, the "check of threes", ask for the pin check,
   walking to the aircraft (prop), the radio check, the dirt dive. Move "Your gear" before "The ride up". **S/M**
9. **Reorder Part IV and the end of the app.** "Act now" chapters first (EP, malfunctions, two canopies, plus a new
   canopy-emergencies chapter). Move "real data", "what went wrong" and "tips" into their own part, "Learn from
   others". Add an always-visible **EP** button in the header. **M**
10. **Short chapter: "after category A: B–H and the A license".** What each category adds, the currency rule (30 days)
    and why to jump AFF in a tight block. It sets expectations and ends the course on what comes next. **S**

## New sections

Each section: where it goes, what it contains (3–6 points), sources. Points marked (DZ) should get a `<q-check/>`
because schools differ.

### 1. Landing somewhere else (Part III, after "The flare" / PLF, before "Landing on an obstacle")
- **Decide early.** At 2,500 ft: can I reach the holding point? By 2,000 ft at the latest, pick an alternate field you
  can reach with height to spare (SIM 4-1 G "Landing Out"; ISP Cat C emergency review).
- **Check it every 500 ft** as you get lower: wires (look for poles along roads and straight tree lines), fences,
  ditches, livestock, slope (ISP Cat C: "inspect the area at every 500-foot interval").
- **Fly a normal, smaller pattern** with a clear final. Into the wind if you can do it without a low turn; a level
  wing comes before direction (SIM 4-1 landing priorities).
- **Ask in the door (later categories):** check the spot and ask for a go-around if it's wrong (SIM 4-1 G). For AFF the
  instructors spot, but the idea explains why the exit point matters.
- **On the ground:** collapse the canopy, check yourself, let the DZ know you're OK (radio, phone, wave to the plane,
  whatever your DZ uses (DZ)), gather the gear, walk back by roads (SIM 4-1 G: "Respect property", "Leave gates as you
  found them", "Avoid walking on crops").
- **Damage:** tell the owner and the DZ; USPA membership insurance covers it (SIM 4-1 G).

### 2. After you land (same chapter, or a short one after PLF)
- Stand up only when the canopy is collapsed. If the wind reinflates it, pull **one toggle (and the steering line) in
  hand over hand** to collapse it; cut away only as a last resort (ISP Cat C emergency review).
- Hurt? Stay down and signal (DZ). Ankle pain after an "OK" landing is common; tell the instructor (BSR G.1.c:
  injuries beyond local first aid are reported).
- Clear the landing area fast; watch for canopies still landing behind you.
- Gather the canopy as you were shown (daisy-chain the lines, or whatever your DZ teaches (DZ)) and **never walk across
  the runway or the aircraft's path** (ISP Cat B "Respect runways and aircraft landing patterns").
- Debrief straight away: watch the video, write the jump in your logbook (ISP; SIM 3-1 logbook).

### 3. Other canopies: rules of the air (Part III, after "Pull, count, check", before the pattern)
- **Three rules:** look before you turn; turn right to avoid; the lower jumper has right of way (Bell, *Safety Check:
  Simple Rules*, USPA 2021).
- **Right after opening:** look around before your first turn, find the other canopies from your load and keep them in
  sight (ISP Cat A canopy check order: altitude, check, look around, then unstow).
- **In the pattern:** most collisions happen below 1,000 ft (SIM 4-1 F). Fly the same pattern as everyone, plan a longer
  base, no S-turns on final, give the lower canopy the right of way.
- **Head-on:** both turn right, unless it's obvious that left is needed (SIM 4-1 F).
- **Can't avoid it:** left arm across the handles, face turned away, legs spread wide (SIM 4-1 F). Tangled: the
  entanglement procedure (SIM 4-1 F; Part IV).
- Visual: see "Visuals to add" #4.

### 4. Wind for students (Part III, first chapter of the canopy part, or Part I "Before you board")
- **Student limit: 14 mph** on a ram-air (BSR 2-1 H.1). Your instructors decide; you can always say "not today".
- **Read the windsock** before you board and again from the air: direction tells you final; how much it is stretched
  tells you the strength (DZ: what "half" and "full" mean on your sock).
- **Ground speed:** facing into the wind you're **holding** (slow over the ground); with the wind you're **running**
  (fast); across it you **crab** (ISP Cat B canopy skills). That's why the holding point is upwind and why final is into
  the wind.
- **Turbulence** reaches 10 to 20 times an obstacle's height on its downwind side: stay away from tree lines, hangars
  and buildings on final (ISP Cat C). In the injury data turbulence was 9% of landing incidents (USPA 2024, already in
  the app).
- **The wind changes on the way down.** If it picks up, face straight into it on final, flare normally, and collapse
  the canopy fast (incident card `x_wind`; ISP Cat C).
- **Drill:** the flight-cycle drill up high (hold ¾ brakes about 5 s, let up, feel the canopy surge for 5–7 s) shows why
  you never let the toggles back up near the ground (ISP Cat C). Category A students don't do it yet, but knowing *why*
  helps the flare rules stick.

### 5. Gear-up and boarding (Part I, first chapter; merge with "Your gear", then "The ride up")
- **SHAGG** before you gear up: Shoes (no hooks), Helmet, Altimeter (zeroed), Goggles (tight), Gloves if used
  (ISP Cat D equipment).
- **Your part of the 4 checks:** handles seated, 3-rings, chest strap threaded and snug, leg straps not twisted,
  RSL connected, AAD on (ask). "Check of threes" before boarding and before exit (ISP Cat D: threes = 3-rings, 3 handles,
  3 straps (DZ: the version your school uses)).
- **Walking to the aircraft:** only with the instructor, stay clear of the propeller, a hand over your handles
  (SIM ISP Cat A aircraft topics; BSR G.1.d: the instructor gear-checks you before boarding).
- **Radio check** before boarding, if your DZ uses radios (DZ). What happens if the radio is silent: you fly your plan
  (see chapter 4 and the existing "radio is a backup" tip).
- **Dirt dive** on the ground with your instructors, the whole dive in order, including the canopy plan with the map
  (ISP Cat A "Category at a Glance").
- **Fit to jump:** no alcohol for 8 h (BSR F), slept and eaten, not sick (blocked ears), current: 30 days off means a
  supervised re-current jump (SIM 4-2). These are in the tips today, but this is where a student looks for them.

### 6. Canopy emergencies (Part IV, after "Two canopies out")
The SIM has this category (SIM 4-1 F); the app has the pieces scattered.
- **Low turn: recovery**, not just avoidance: both toggles to bring the canopy back overhead, stop the turn, flare from
  where you are, PLF (SIM 4-1 F). Also expert part III #5.
- **Collision / entanglement:** the posture above, then entanglement with another jumper: altitude decides (SIM 4-1 F).
- **Dropped toggle / broken steering line near the ground:** steer and flare with the rear risers (ISP Cat A canopy
  emergencies (DZ: whether your school teaches it on Cat A)). The tips say a dropped toggle caused "the most severe
  injuries" of 2024 landings, but no chapter says what to do.
- **The reserve is a canopy too:** after a cutaway, run the same check, find the DZ, fly a normal pattern, PLF. Don't
  chase your main or freebag (SIM 4-1 E; Part IV "two parachutes" theme).
- **Premature opening in freefall:** find the pilot chute, two tries or 5 s, then cut away and reserve (SIM 4-1 E).
  Today only the horseshoe card covers it.

### 7. Water landing: complete the steps (existing obstacles chapter)
Already flagged by expert part III #3. From a learning point of view, make it a short ordered sequence (a `steps`
block), not a card, because order matters: steer away → loosen the chest strap → disconnect the RSL → half brakes at
10 ft → stay in the harness until you're in the water → cut away → arms back, swim forward out (SIM 4-1 G Water
Landings).

### 8. Four survival skills (first screen + printable card)
- **Altitude:** know it all the time; check before and after every maneuver and whenever in doubt.
- **Pull:** pull; at your altitude; stable if you can.
- **Rules of the air:** look before you turn, turn right, the lower jumper has right of way.
- **Landing:** level wing flying straight; clear open area; flare (at least half brakes) and PLF.
Source: Bell, *Safety Check: Simple Rules*, USPA, 24 May 2021; SIM 4-1. Note for the fact-checkers: that column says
altitude "every five seconds or every 1,000 feet"; the app says USPA = every 3 s. One of them should win before this
card ships.

### 9. After category A: B–H and the A license (Part V or a closing chapter)
- **What each category adds:** B pull on your own, leg control, HAALR; C first release, control within 5 s,
  flight-cycle drill; D solo exit, turns, rear risers, four supervised gear checks; E–H tracking, group jumps with a
  coach, packing, spotting (ISP category pages B, C, D, G).
- **Pass criteria early on:** Cat A pull within 1,000 ft of the planned altitude, Cat B within 500 ft, Cat C at the
  assigned altitude (Sharp, Rating Corner, Parachutist, July 2019; already in the tips).
- **A license:** 25 jumps, card, check dive, written (75%+) and oral exam (USPA A-license page; already in the tips).
- **Currency:** 30 days without a jump means a supervised re-current jump (SIM 4-2). Jump AFF in a tight block.
- Move the "Categories with instructors A → E" card from the Skydive California chapter here.

### 10. Radio and ground signals (fold into chapter 4 or "after you land")
- The radio helps; it doesn't command. A wrong or late call (or a dropout) has caused high flares (`injury-data.md`).
- No calls at all: fly your plan; flare on your own judgment.
- Ground-to-air backup: the DZ must have "manned ground-to-air communications" (BSR J.3). Ask what yours uses if the
  radio fails (arrow, paddles, a person waving (DZ)), and how you acknowledge calls (often a leg kick (DZ)).

## New test questions

Format: question / options (★ = right answer) / why / source. All are decisions, not recall. Add `<q-check/>` where
the answer depends on the school.

**Plane and gear**
1. *The main container pops open in the plane, near the open door.* Hold it closed and tell the instructor ★ / Throw
   it out of the door / Sit on it and keep quiet. Why: keep it in; if it goes out the door you go right after it. The
   app's callout says this but no question tests it. SIM 4-1 E; ISP Cat C.
2. *Nobody has checked your gear since you put it on, and the load is walking to the plane.* Ask your instructor for a
   check before you board ★ / Board, they'll check in the plane / Check it yourself and go. Why: instructors do a full
   gear check before boarding (BSR G.1.d), and that's one of the four. Replaces "How many times is your gear checked?".
3. *The windsock is standing straight out and gusting while you gear up.* Ask the instructor; students have a 14 mph
   limit ★ / Jump; the canopy flies faster in wind / Jump, but land downwind. BSR 2-1 H.1.

**Freefall**
4. *5,500 ft and you're turning slowly, not quite stable.* Pull now ★ / Get stable first, then pull / Wait for an
   instructor to stop you. Why: pull priorities: pull, at your altitude, stable if you can. Bell 2021; incident card
   `x_aff`. Replaces "How many practice touches?".
5. *First reach for the pilot chute: your hand finds nothing.* Second try: sweep along the bottom of the container ★ /
   Pull the reserve at once / Look back over your shoulder for it. Why: two tries or 5 s, then the emergency procedure
   (ISP Cat B emergency review; SIM 4-1 E).
6. *8,000 ft: you can't see either instructor.* — already in the tips quiz; **move it into the dive-flow quiz**, where
   it belongs.

**Canopy**
7. *Right after opening, another canopy at your height is heading straight at you.* Turn right ★ / Turn left / Hold
   still and let them avoid you. SIM 4-1 F; Bell 2021.
8. *In the pattern you're above another student, both going to the target.* They have right of way: give them room ★ /
   You do, you're higher / Whoever is faster. Bell 2021 ("the lower jumper has the right-of-way").
9. *2,000 ft, short of the holding point; facing into the wind you barely move over the ground.* Pick a clear field you
   can reach and plan your pattern there ★ / Push on to the target; you'll make it / Turn downwind to go faster, then
   turn back low. SIM 4-1 G; ISP Cat C.
10. *Canopy check: rectangle, slider halfway up, end cells closed, 4,500 ft.* Altitude, then flare and hold, then
    check again ★ / Cut away now / Wait and see. Why: minor problems usually clear during the controllability check;
    if not, act by 2,500 ft (ISP Cat A). Replaces "What does square mean?".
11. *Radio silent at 1,500 ft, your plan is good.* Fly your plan and flare on your own judgment ★ / Circle until the
    radio calls you / Go to the holding point and wait. Why: the radio is a backup (app tips; `injury-data.md`).
12. *100 ft: you pulled one toggle too far and the canopy is diving.* Both toggles to bring it back overhead, stop the
    turn, flare from there, PLF ★ / Let go of both toggles / Pull the other toggle hard to turn back. SIM 4-1 F Low Turns.
13. *A dropped toggle at 200 ft (toggle out of reach).* Steer and flare with the rear risers ★ / Reach up for the
    toggle and look at it / Cut away. (DZ) ISP Cat A canopy emergencies.

**Landing and after**
14. *You've landed and the wind reinflates the canopy and drags you.* Pull one toggle in hand over hand until it
    collapses ★ / Cut away right away / Hold both risers and stand up. ISP Cat C.
15. *You land in a farmer's field.* Collapse the canopy, tell the DZ you're OK, gather the gear, leave gates as found,
    don't walk on crops ★ / Leave the gear and walk back for help / Cut through the field to the DZ. SIM 4-1 G.
16. *About to land in water, no flotation, 300 ft.* Loosen the chest strap and disconnect the RSL; half brakes at 10 ft;
    out of the harness once you're in the water ★ / Unbuckle the leg straps up high / Keep the canopy to float on.
    SIM 4-1 G. Replaces the current water question, which misses the RSL step.
17. *Collision can't be avoided.* Left arm across the handles, face away, legs wide ★ / Grab the other jumper /
    Cut away at once. SIM 4-1 F.

**Emergencies**
18. *After the cutaway the reserve opens with line twists.* Risers apart and kick, toggles stowed, same as the main ★ /
    Cut away the reserve / Pull the toggles to stop the turn. Why: the reserve flies like the main (app, Part IV). Tests
    transfer, not recall.
19. *You cut away at 3,000 ft; the main and freebag fall away below you.* Steer the reserve to the DZ, fly a normal
    pattern ★ / Follow the main to see where it lands / Land next to the main to pick it up. "Never save the gear"
    (app) applied to a new case.

## Reorder / cut

### Order
- **Part I:** gear-up and boarding (new #5, including "Your gear") → "The ride up" → climb-out → exit count. Today
  "The ride up" comes before the gear you put on before boarding.
- **Part III:** pull, count, check → **other canopies** (new) → wind (new, or in Part I) → pattern → flare + PLF →
  **landing somewhere else / after you land** (new) → obstacles. Today obstacles sit between the pattern and the flare,
  which breaks the jump order of a normal landing. Obstacles are the exception, so they go last.
- **Part IV (act now):** EP → what can go wrong → two canopies out → canopy emergencies (new). Keep it short and
  scannable; this is the part a student opens in a hurry.
- **New Part, "Learn from others"** (read at home): how people actually get hurt → what went wrong: real jumps →
  common mistakes and tips. These are reflection, not procedure, and today they bury the EP chapters.
- **Part V:** your dropzone → ask the instructors (moved here, next to the DZ facts it complements) → after category A
  (new #9) → read and watch next.
- **Header:** an always-visible red **EP** pill that jumps to `#ep`. The first screen has the emergency numbers, but
  once you've scrolled there's no fast way back.
- **Ground-school test (Q26–32):** it's your school's test, so move it to Part V, or show it as a "test" card after the
  flare. It doesn't need to be a chapter inside the canopy part.

### Cut or fold away
- **"Mistakes and tips" chapter (~60 bullets):** it repeats most chapters. Put the 2–3 most important mistakes as a
  `tips` block at the end of each matching chapter, and fold the full list into a collapsed "more" block. The
  "Getting through AFF" group moves to the new "after category A" chapter.
- **Injury bars:** keep "when in the jump" and "what went wrong at landing"; fold "which part of the body" and
  "inside the leg". They don't change what a student does.
- **"Heard on the course, not sure what it meant" callout in the EP chapter:** an unanswered question in the most
  critical chapter adds doubt at the worst moment. Keep it only in "ask the instructors".
- **Duplicate watch links** (two signal videos from the same school; two flare/PLF links next to clips that already
  show the move): keep one per chapter.
- **SDCA quiz** (see the table above): cut.
- **Pattern list:** "check the windsock before you board" appears twice (expert part III #17).
- Keep the four 2,500 ft callouts ("same number, different job") but use **one** design for all of them. The
  confusion is real (the 1,200-jump case), and a single recognisable visual teaches it better than four wordings.
