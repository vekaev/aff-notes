# Skydiving safety project: worldwide foundation directory

Research date: 16 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles. No one was contacted.

Selected scope: worldwide federations, reusable directories/datasets, and up to ten dropzone leads per country or territory. The CSV contains **533 rows**, covering **116 countries and territories**, plus worldwide sources. **278 rows have a published email**: 195 from the organisation's own site and 83 from a public directory or official third-party document. 100 rows have an inquiry-form URL. An email appearing on a website does not establish deliverability or current operating status.

The dropzone selections are **unranked**. Comparable annual jump totals, student throughput or fleet statistics were not available across countries, so a defensible “ten biggest per country” ranking could not be produced. Large training centres were prioritised where identifiable; smaller-market listings were retained to broaden coverage. Directory-only leads remain in the CSV with low or medium confidence and explicit uncertainty notes.

The main global map yielded **880 raw records across 80 normalised country/territory labels**. All 80 labels have representation in this directory, with additional dropzone geographies recovered from another directory. That is 100% geographic representation of this particular seed map, not 100% coverage of worldwide operators. The map contains duplicates and historical entries. Of 379 initially selected map records, 374 were retained; six directly checked additions and 21 additional-country leads bring the dropzone total to 401.

Useful national comparisons: the APF public search returned 37 operator rows, versus ten Australian leads here; British Skydiving describes 23 locations, versus ten UK leads here; DFV exposes 99 club/operator records, including an apparent test record, versus ten German leads here. These are different units and do not establish market-share coverage. PASA displays 14 dropzones, ten marked for student training; this directory's ten South African selections are not a complete reconciliation of those 14. A reliable global percentage cannot be calculated from these overlapping, inconsistent inventories.

## Creative searches: results

1. **Manifest software customers — worked.** Burble exposes a useful 163-listing cross-check, including smaller operations. It reflects customers, not the whole market.
2. **Manufacturer partner lists — worked.** CYPRES supplied 12 association links, useful for validating federation names and finding alternate routes.
3. **Skydiver logbook-app catalogues — worked as leads.** JumpLogs and Skytime broadened discovery, including smaller markets; published totals and data licences remained unclear.
4. **Official competition bulletins — worked.** FAI-hosted event PDFs exposed designated Indonesian skydiving-committee and Thai national-body contacts. These remain medium confidence because the email evidence is third-party documentation.
5. **Aeronautical information publications — worked for historical leads.** The Irish ENR 5.5 extract exposes seasonal activity absent from typical travel lists; it requires a current-status check.
6. **Open-source skydiving software — worked.** SSScoring contained an actual 251-row coordinate CSV. Other repositories supplied source code or live-data approaches rather than a verified reusable inventory.
7. **OpenStreetMap tagging — worked partially.** The 206 tagged objects are a useful geospatial cross-check, but duplicates, missing tags and airfield-level objects prevent treating them as a definitive DZ count.
8. **World Skydiving Day participant pages — limited.** The current [locations page](https://www.worldskydivingday.com/locations) invites participation without exposing a useful complete operator roster.
9. **Wikidata / Kaggle / data.world — no usable complete list found.** Searches produced insufficiently relevant or incomplete results; no data was fabricated to fill the gap.

Local-language searches covered, among others, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, Scandinavian languages, Ukrainian, Russian and Japanese. Coverage was not equally deep in every language or country.

## Quality controls and gaps

Every CSV row has a source URL. Emails were taken from visible public text, public mailto links, publicly rendered email obfuscation or published documents. No email was generated from an address pattern. High means email on the organisation's own site; medium means a public directory/third-party document; low means no email verification. A low-confidence row may still have a valid official inquiry form. Languages describe observed site/contact language, not a promise of staff fluency. Audience counts are blank because this run does not cover creator/community audiences.

Similar names were manually reviewed before matching. Cross-country false matches were rejected. The Skydive Ireland entry was reassigned to Northern Ireland/UK using its location. A supposed Kazakhstan Skydive Ural entry was rejected after the operator's own site placed it in Russia. Duplicated BRO Indonesia, Cebu and Aarhus/Århus listings were removed. Shared corporate contacts and separately named branches remain separate where the source identifies distinct entries. Historical aliases may still conceal additional duplicates among unverified listings.

The largest gap is **verification depth**: many historical listings lack a reachable own site or current contact. A blank website/email means it was not established, not that none exists. The CSV preserves historical Ukrainian DZ leads with current operation explicitly unverified. Suspended federation memberships are noted where observed. An unreachable website alone was not treated as proof of closure.

For the next pass, reconcile each national federation's full current operator roster against the global seeds, obtain public annual jump-volume data where available, and verify small, military, university and seasonal clubs individually. Countries with only a general FAI air-sport body need a published referral to their actual parachuting authority. Names in association contact rows should be checked again before use because office-holders change.

The selected foundation scope does not inventory standalone tunnels, gear businesses, coaches, researchers, creators or online communities. Public group posting rules and membership counts therefore remain for the dedicated communities run. Private groups, logins and paywalls were not used.
