# AFF stages 5–7: Deployment, Canopy Check, Malfunctions & Emergency Procedures

Context: AFF student, pull altitude 5,500 ft. All text paraphrased. Researched 2026-11-16. USPA's web SIM now files emergencies under **Chapter 4, Section 4-1 "Skydiving Emergencies"** (older material, including the brief, calls it Section 5-1), and the student program (ISP) lives under uspa.org/skydiveschool/{A..H}.

## Q1. Stage 5 — Deployment: mistakes, tips, videos

### Takeaway
USPA Category A teaches: lock on to the altimeter at 6,000 ft, wave off at 5,500 ft, then a slow, symmetrical wave-arch-reach-pull. Count to 3,000 and look for the pilot chute and canopy. A hard pull or a lost handle gets **no more than two tries or 5 seconds**, then emergency procedures start. The AAD is only a backup: pulling is always the jumper's job.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequence (USPA Cat A):** lock on at 6,000 ft and wave off with both arms overhead at 5,500 ft. The wave-off signals altitude awareness and intent to pull. The wave-off, arch, reach and pull take about 1,000 ft. — [USPA ISP Category A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Body position during the pull:** keep the spine straight and the body symmetrical, stretch the free hand overhead for balance, throw the handle briskly, then return to a neutral arch. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Counting:** after the throw, count by thousands to 3,000, then look for pilot-chute and main deployment. IAD and static-line students count to five. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Lost handle:** sweep across the bottom of the container, then down the side to the corner. Stop after two tries or 5 seconds and go to emergency procedures. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Hard pull:** two tries or 5 seconds, then emergency procedures. Category B adds bracing the elbow against the container for leverage. Category C academics suggest a second try with two hands. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A); [USPA Cat B](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/b); [USPA Cat C](https://uspa.org/skydiveschool/C) (via search summary)
- **SIM rule:** a hard pull or a missing handle counts as a total malfunction. Try only if altitude allows, at most two tries or 5 more seconds. If no main pilot chute comes out, deploy the reserve. — [USPA SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- **Pilot-chute hesitation** (the pilot chute stays in the burble): twist at the waist and look over the shoulder to change the airflow, at most two tries or 5 seconds. **Entanglement:** arch harder and point the tangled limb at the sky. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **AAD:** it fires the reserve at a set altitude and speed, but USPA stresses it is only a backup. Pulling the handles is always the jumper's job. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Example of what goes wrong:** a Category F student was distracted by goggles at about 6,000 ft and stared at her altimeter. She went head-low and made a weak pilot-chute throw with one leg out of position. The pilot chute wrapped her leg and she froze. Her AAD fired the reserve at about 700 ft. — [USPA Rating Corner, EP Proficiency, R. Bell, 2023-03-09](https://www.uspa.org/about-uspa/uspa-news/rating-corner-emergency-procedure-proficiency)
- **Instructor tip:** a student can pull perfectly nine times and then freeze on the tenth. If a student forgets the dive flow, or looks away during lock-on, expect them not to pull. — [USPA Rating Corner, Tips for New AFF Instructors](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-tips-for-new-aff-instructors)
- **Instructor tip:** if a student forgets the practice touches on the first jump, they may have forgotten everything. — [USPA Rating Corner, Tips for New AFF Instructors](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-tips-for-new-aff-instructors) (via search summary)

**Common student mistakes (from the sources above):**
1. Losing altitude awareness: watching the camera flyer or instructor instead of the altimeter, or fixating on one thing. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A); [Rating Corner 2023](https://www.uspa.org/about-uspa/uspa-news/rating-corner-emergency-procedure-proficiency)
2. Pulling late or hesitating at 5,500 ft. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
3. An asymmetrical, head-low or unstable body during the reach, which leads to a weak throw and a pilot chute tangled on a limb. — [Rating Corner 2023](https://www.uspa.org/about-uspa/uspa-news/rating-corner-emergency-procedure-proficiency)
4. Skipping the practice touches, or forgetting the dive flow. — [Tips for New AFF Instructors](https://www.uspa.org/rating-corner-tips-for-new-aff-instructors)
5. Spending more than two tries or 5 seconds on a hard pull or a missing handle. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
6. Counting on the AAD. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)

**Practical tips:**
- Keep the order wave, arch, reach, pull, throw and return to the arch, with the free arm overhead for balance. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- Count out loud ("one thousand… three thousand"), then check. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- Hard pull: brace the elbow on the container, try again with two hands, and stop at two tries or 5 seconds. — [Cat B](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/b); [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- Hesitation: look over the shoulder and twist. This is the one moment when looking over the shoulder is correct. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A); [USPA malfunction series, PC in tow](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)

### Inferences
- The brief mentions "not looking over the shoulder too early". I found no USPA text on this. The ISP only says to keep the body symmetrical and to look over the shoulder only for a hesitation. That turning and dropping a shoulder during the pull upsets stability is my inference from the "symmetrical" instruction.

### Gaps
- I found no public video with a verified timestamp that shows a correct AFF wave-off and pull sequence.
- I found no Reddit thread focused on deployment mistakes. Reddit mirrors gave only scattered anecdotes, so I did not use them.

## Q2. Stage 6 — Canopy check and controllability

### Takeaway
USPA teaches "Is it there? Is it square? Is it steerable?" (some dropzones say "stable" in place of "there"). Next comes a controllability check with the toggles unstowed: straight flight, a full flare and a smooth return to full flight, then turns of at least 90° each way. The canopy must be good by **2,500 ft**, otherwise start emergency procedures. Line twists, closed end cells and a slider stuck high are usually fixable, but if in doubt, start emergency procedures.

### Cited Findings
- **The questions:** there / square / steerable, with some programs using "stable" in place of "there". "Square" means four clean edges, lines in neat groups, the slider down at the risers, and level flight without turning or spinning. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Controllability check:** unstow the toggles, check straight flight, do a full flare and return smoothly to full flight, then turn at least 90° each way and stop each turn. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Student decision altitude:** 2,500 ft. If the canopy is not safe and controllable by then, start emergency procedures right away. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Minor problems:** closed end cells, a slider stuck high or an unwanted turn usually clear during the controllability check. If the problem stays or gets worse, start emergency procedures by 2,500 ft. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Line twists:** they are common and usually easy. Spread the risers and kick, and **release the brakes only after the twists clear**. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Canopy turns on its own after twists clear:** check that both brakes are released, because one toggle may have come unstowed. — [USPA malfunction video series (Skydive Mag)](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Broken lines or damage:** run the there/square/steerable check. Cut away if more than one suspension line is broken, if a hole is head-sized, or if sky shows through the bottom skin. — [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Snivel:** modern canopies take about 5 seconds to inflate, sometimes up to 10. If it is not fully inflated by 2,500 ft, cut away. — [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Flare practice and landing:** start the flare at about twice your height and finish with arms down at about 1–3 ft. Letting the toggles up sharply makes the canopy dive. "You go where your eyes go." — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **Airspace / canopy collisions:** the usual rule is that both jumpers turn right. If they are tangled and too low to cut away, both deploy reserves. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **"Halfway down, halfway back"** check for spotting an off-landing early. — [Skydive Mag, "When, Not If", R. Bell 2021](https://www.skydivemag.com/new?p=32958)

**Common student mistakes:**
1. Releasing the brakes, or pulling a toggle, while still in line twists. This can turn twists into a spin. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
2. Spending too long on a "minor" problem that is getting worse, and passing 2,500 ft. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
3. Skipping the controllability check (flare plus turns), so an unflyable canopy is found only on landing. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
4. Thinking a canopy that was landable stays landable. Damage, a collision or a worsening problem can come later. — [Parachutist Safety Check, R. Bell, 2024-11-08](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/ArtMID/2834/ArticleID/9488)
5. Not checking the sky for other canopies.

**Practical tips:**
- Order: altitude, there/square/steerable, look around for traffic, unstow, flare, turns, then altitude again. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- Fix line twists first, then release the brakes.
- If in doubt, start emergency procedures. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)

### Inferences
- Pumping the brakes to open end cells and clear a stuck slider is widely taught, but the USPA sources here only say "controllability check". The exact pumping technique is something to confirm with the dropzone.
- "Check airspace" in the brief has only partial support: collision avoidance (turn right) and lookout are in Cat A. I found no separate formal "airspace check" step.

### Gaps
- I found no specific video with a verified timestamp of a student controllability check (flare plus turns).

## Q3. Stage 7 — Malfunctions, EPs, decision altitude, what kills people, how to drill

### Takeaway
**USPA's current student decision altitude is 2,500 ft** (SIM 4-1: students and A-license holders decide and act no lower than 2,500 ft). Separately, the **cutaway hard deck is 1,000 ft**. Below it, do not cut away: deploy the reserve and land both canopies. The one exception is a downplane, which is cut away at any altitude. Deaths and injuries come mostly from **hesitation and delay** (especially with spinning malfunctions), **lost altitude awareness**, **cutting away too low**, and **not pulling the reserve after a cutaway**. The remedies are the 5-second rule, two hands on the handles, and practice in a hanging harness that goes all the way to landing.

### Decision altitude — exact sources
- SIM Section 4-1 "Skydiving Emergencies": students and A-license holders act by a predetermined altitude **no lower than 2,500 ft**. — [USPA SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- ISP Category A: student decision altitude is 2,500 ft. — [USPA ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- Ron Bell, USPA Director of Safety and Training (Parachutist Safety Check, 2024-11-08): the SIM figure is no lower than 2,500 ft for students and A-license holders. Decision altitude ≠ cutaway hard deck (usually 1,000 ft). Cutting away is still possible between them. — [Parachutist 2024](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/ArtMID/2834/ArticleID/9488)
- **Discrepancy for licensed jumpers:** Bell 2024 gives 1,800 ft for B–D, while the current SIM web text (per search summary) gives 2,000 ft for B and C. This does not affect students (2,500 for all). — [Parachutist 2024](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/ArtMID/2834/ArticleID/9488); [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- **Dropzone differences:** pull altitudes differ (for example, Cat A pull at 5,500 for AFF, wave-off at 6,000 for tandem). Some instructors advise students not to open below 4,000 ft. Many non-US schools (for example, those under BPA or other national systems) use their own figures. Check with your dropzone. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A); [Parachutist, Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)

### Malfunction types and actions (USPA)
- **Total** (the pin is not out or the container is closed): hard pull, lost handle, pilot chute in tow. **Partial** (the container is open but the canopy is not landable): bag lock, streamer, line-over, pilot-chute entanglement, premature deployment, tension knots, major damage. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- **Partial malfunction:** cut away, then the reserve. Below 1,000 ft, reserve only. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- **Pilot chute in tow:** there are two accepted options. Pull the reserve straight away, or cut away and then pull the reserve. Students should follow what their instructor or S&TA teaches, because **dropzones differ** here. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1); [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Line-over, bag lock:** cut away and pull the reserve by at least 2,500 ft. — [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Horseshoe / premature deployment:** point the tangled limb up, at most two tries, then cut away and pull the reserve. — [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/); [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
- **Line twists, non-spinning:** spread the risers and kick, or squeeze them together and unwind your body. **Spinning or diving and not under the canopy:** cut away and pull the reserve by 2,500 ft. — [USPA malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
- **Two canopies out:** for a biplane or side-by-side, land both and steer gently, then PLF. For a downplane, cut away at any altitude. For a main-reserve entanglement, keep clearing it, pull in the less-inflated canopy hand over hand, and PLF. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1); [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
- **"Two tries" rule:** applies to fixable problems (hard pull, lost handle, hesitation, horseshoe, premature opening). The limit is two tries or 5 seconds. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A); [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)

### Cutaway sequence and technique
- Bell (2019) cites the mantra "look, grab, cut away". He also recommends two hands on the handle: peel up to clear the Velcro, then punch down to full arm extension. Several jumpers found a one-handed cutaway failed under spin G-forces. — [Parachutist, "Don't Delay, Cut Away! — Spinning Malfunctions and Difficult Cutaways", Bell, 2019-07-01](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)
- The full "look red, look silver; peel/punch red; pull silver; arch" sequence is the common dropzone version. USPA sources here describe its parts (look, grab, two hands, peel and punch, then the reserve) but do not spell it out word for word. Some dropzones teach a one-hand-per-handle method. **Check which variant your dropzone teaches.**
- **Do not rely on the AAD after a cutaway.** After a release, the jumper has to speed back up toward freefall speed before the AAD will fire. Pull the reserve yourself, right away. — [Parachutist 2017 Fatality Summary](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/artmid/2830/articleid/8636) (via search summary)
- **RSL/MARD:** analyses of fatalities say an RSL would likely have prevented several low-cutaway deaths. Student rigs usually have an RSL. — [Parachutist, Risk Assessment & Decision Making](https://parachutist.com/Article/super-sis-55) (via search summary); [Parachutist 2017 Fatality Summary](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/artmid/2830/articleid/8636)

### Which errors kill and injure
- **Delay and hesitation:** delayed decisions are a leading cause of deaths. Failing to cut away and pull the reserve in time was a major factor in recent deaths, although release and reserve systems rarely fail. — [Parachutist, "Don't Delay, Cut Away! — The Five-Second Rule", B. Planche Wallace, 2019-07-01](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/6/artmid/2830/articleid/7485)
- **Spinning malfunctions:** of 5 US deaths by mid-May 2019, 4 involved spinning malfunctions. Bell says all 4 could have been avoided by recognizing early that things were not improving and acting at once. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)
- **Why spins are dangerous:** altitude is lost fast, the jumper is disoriented, G-forces make the handles harder to pull, the main lift web pinches the cutaway pocket, and twisted risers can keep you from looking at the handles. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)
- **Statistic:** per USPA data cited in the article, no US jumper in the previous 10 years died solely of a spinning malfunction when emergency procedures were done at or above decision altitude within about 5 seconds. — [Planche Wallace 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/6/artmid/2830/articleid/7485)
- **Statistic:** more than 4,000 cutaways a year in the US, about one per 700–800 jumps. — [Planche Wallace 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/6/artmid/2830/articleid/7485)
- **Low cutaway / low reserve:** a thesis-based analysis found that all 8 deaths in its "no or low cutaway/reserve" category involved lost situational awareness and no RSL. In 6 the reserve was deployed too low to inflate, and in 2 the main was cut away too low. — [Parachutist, Risk Assessment, Decision Making and Skydiving Safety](https://parachutist.com/Article/super-sis-55) (via search summary)
- **Too low for the reserve to get overhead:** in a 2017 case, the jumper released the main but was too low for the reserve to get overhead and slow the fall. — [Parachutist 2017 Fatality Summary](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/artmid/2830/articleid/8636) (via search summary)
- **Freezing:** stress can cause fight, flight or freeze. Feeling comfortable after one harness session is not proficiency. — [Rating Corner 2023](https://www.uspa.org/about-uspa/uspa-news/rating-corner-emergency-procedure-proficiency)
- **Confusing decision altitude with the hard deck:** a jumper with 1,200+ jumps did not cut away at 2,100 ft because he thought he was past the point where cutting away was allowed. — [Parachutist 2024](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/ArtMID/2834/ArticleID/9488)
- **Monday-morning quarterbacking:** second-guessing other people's cutaways makes jumpers hesitate in future emergencies. Say "good job" first and debrief later. — [Parachutist Safety Check "This Isn't Football – Don't Delay, Cut Away!", M. Schock, 2025](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/artmid/2830/articleid/9555) (via search summary; full text not opened)

**Common EP mistakes (summary):**
1. Looking for the handles late, or not looking at all, and grabbing at random. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)
2. Hesitating or trying to "save" the main past 5 seconds or past 2,500 ft. — [Planche Wallace 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/6/artmid/2830/articleid/7485); [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1)
3. Trying to kick out of twists under a spinning canopy. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486); [malfunction series](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/uspa-malfunctions-video-series/)
4. Releasing the brakes while in twists. — [ISP Cat A](https://www.uspa.org/skydiveschool/A)
5. A one-handed, weak pull of the cutaway handle that does not clear the Velcro. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)
6. Cutting away below 1,000 ft. — [SIM 4-1](https://www.uspa.org/sim/4-1); [Parachutist 2017 summary](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/artmid/2830/articleid/8636)
7. Not pulling the reserve after cutting away and counting on the RSL or AAD. — [Parachutist 2017 summary](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/artmid/2830/articleid/8636)
8. Mixing up decision altitude and the hard deck. — [Parachutist 2024](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/160/ArtMID/2834/ArticleID/9488)

### How instructors drill
- **The 5-second rule:** once a malfunction is spotted, give yourself about 5 seconds to decide. If things are clearly improving (for example, twists are unwinding), continue in 5-second blocks and rarely go past 15 seconds. If they are the same or worse, act. Count consciously. — [Planche Wallace 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/6/artmid/2830/articleid/7485)
- **Hanging harness, all the way to the ground:** run full scenarios through the cutaway and the reserve, at least 3 times per session. Add a second problem under the reserve (twists, a slider more than halfway up). The instructor calls out altitude and position ("1,500 ft, a mile from the DZ"). The student **says out loud** what they do next. The instructor adds stress by shouting about a hazard ("power lines!"). — [Bell, "When, Not If", 2021](https://www.skydivemag.com/new?p=32958)
- **Practice under load:** handles sit higher on the chest when the harness is loaded, so practicing on the ground without a hanging harness is less useful. — [search summary: Parachutist Safety Check "EPs and Your Gear"](https://parachutist.com/Article/ArtMID/2847/ArticleID/7451/Safety-check - EPs-and-Your-Gear) (not opened in full)
- **Repeat before every jump**, until it is automatic, not just until you feel "comfortable". — [Rating Corner 2023](https://www.uspa.org/about-uspa/uspa-news/rating-corner-emergency-procedure-proficiency)
- **Two hands, peel and punch, look first.** AFF instructors stress the basics, which saved even experienced jumpers. — [Bell 2019](https://parachutist.com/p/PgrID/2839/PageID/9/ArtMID/2830/ArticleID/7486)

### Inferences
- For an AFF student pulling at 5,500 ft: if the canopy is fully open at about 4,500 ft, there are about 2,000 ft until 2,500. That is enough for the checks and one or two fix attempts, but not for a long "rescue". Under a spin, altitude goes much faster.
- The pilot-chute-in-tow procedure and the exact cutaway technique (one hand vs. two, look red/look silver) differ between dropzones. Follow your own dropzone's procedure.
